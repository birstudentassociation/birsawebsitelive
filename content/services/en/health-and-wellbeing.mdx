Looking after your health is easier when you already know where to go before you need it. Here's a quick signpost to Thammasat's services, plus numbers worth saving in your phone today.

## University health services

Thammasat provides a student health centre for routine care — the per-semester health fee (see the fees guide) contributes toward this. For anything beyond routine care, the university can typically direct you to affiliated hospitals, including Thammasat University Hospital.

## Counselling and mental health support

Thammasat's student affairs office runs counselling services for students dealing with stress, adjustment difficulties, or anything else affecting their wellbeing. These conversations are confidential — you don't need a diagnosis or a crisis to make an appointment, and reaching out early is normal, not a last resort.

If you're not sure where to start, the BIR office can point you to the right counselling contact, or you can ask BIRSA in confidence and we'll help you find the way in.

## Emergency numbers

Save these now, not when you need them:

| Situation | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Medical emergency | 1669 |
| Police | 191 |

If you're an international student unsure how emergency services work in Thailand, it's worth asking a Thai-speaking friend or the BIR office to walk you through it before you ever need to use it.

## A note from BIRSA

We're students, not medical professionals — this page is a signpost, not a substitute for the university's own health services. If you're struggling, please use the university services above, or talk to someone you trust. BIRSA can also help point you toward the right office if you're not sure who to ask.
