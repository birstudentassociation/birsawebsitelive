The university has announced that from **1 August 2026** all students must have multi-factor authentication (MFA) switched on for their university account. MFA adds a second check on top of your password, so someone who steals your password still cannot get into your email, your files, or the registrar's system.

<Notice variant="warning" title="Do this before 1 August 2026">
  Set MFA up now, while you have time and a working internet connection. If you leave it until the
  deadline you may be locked out of the systems you need on the day you need them.
</Notice>

## What you need before you start

- Your Thammasat email address, which looks like `yourname.abc@dome.tu.ac.th`, and its password.
- Your phone, with enough storage to install one small app.
- A computer, or a second device. It is much easier to scan the setup QR code with your phone while the code is on a different screen.

You can do the whole thing on the phone alone, but the two-device route is smoother, so this guide assumes a computer plus a phone.

## Step 1: Install Microsoft Authenticator on your phone

Open the App Store (iPhone) or Google Play (Android) and search for **Microsoft Authenticator**. The publisher must be **Microsoft Corporation**. There are many apps with similar names and icons, so check the publisher before you install.

Open the app once it is installed. Accept the privacy prompt, and skip the sign-in screen if it offers one. You do not add your account from inside the app; the next steps will send it there.

## Step 2: Open your security info page

On your computer, go to <a href="https://aka.ms/mfasetup">aka.ms/mfasetup</a>.

Sign in with your full `@dome.tu.ac.th` address and your password. You land on a page titled **Security info**, which lists the ways you can prove it is you.

<Notice title="Signed in as the wrong account?">
  If the page shows a personal Microsoft account or another university, use your browser's private
  or incognito window and sign in again with your `@dome.tu.ac.th` address.
</Notice>

## Step 3: Add Microsoft Authenticator as a sign-in method

1. Select **Add sign-in method**.
2. Choose **Authenticator app** from the list.
3. Select **Add**. Microsoft shows a short "Start by getting the app" screen. You already have it, so select **Next**.
4. On the "Set up your account" screen, select **Next** again. A **QR code** appears on your computer screen. Leave it there.

## Step 4: Scan the QR code with the app

On your phone, in Microsoft Authenticator:

1. Tap the **+** button, usually at the top right.
2. Choose **Work or school account**.
3. Choose **Scan QR code**. Allow camera access if the phone asks.
4. Point the camera at the QR code on your computer screen.

Your account appears in the app's list as `yourname.abc@dome.tu.ac.th`. That is the pairing done.

## Step 5: Approve the test sign-in

Back on the computer, select **Next**. Microsoft sends a test notification to your phone and shows a **two-digit number** on the computer screen.

Open the notification on your phone, type that number into the app, and tap **Yes** or **Approve**. The computer confirms with **Notification approved**.

<Notice>
  Typing a number from the screen is normal and correct. If you ever get an approval request when
  you are not signing in to anything, tap **No, it's not me** and change your password.
</Notice>

## Step 6: Add a backup method

Do not skip this. If you lose your phone, break it, or wipe it, a backup method is what keeps you out of the helpdesk queue.

On the **Security info** page, select **Add sign-in method** again and add **Phone**. Enter your mobile number with the country code, for example `+66`, and confirm the code that arrives by SMS.

While you are there, check the **Default sign-in method** near the top of the page. Set it to **Microsoft Authenticator - notification**, which is faster and safer than an SMS code.

## What changes when you sign in from now on

You will still type your password as usual. After that, roughly once per device and browser, Microsoft will send an approval to your phone with a two-digit number to type in. Approve it and you are through. On your own laptop you can tick **Don't ask again for 60 days** when it is offered.

## If something goes wrong

- **No notification arrives.** Open Microsoft Authenticator manually and look for a pending request. If there is none, check that notifications are enabled for the app in your phone settings.
- **The app shows a code but nothing to approve.** Tap your account in the app to see the six-digit code, and type that code into the sign-in screen instead.
- **You changed phones.** Set the new phone up before you wipe the old one. If the old phone is already gone, sign in with your backup phone number and add the app again from the security info page.
- **You are locked out completely.** Contact the university's Office of Information and Communication Technology. They take support requests by email at <Email address="ict.tu@tu.ac.th" /> and on LINE at `@icttuhelpdesk`.

## Why the university is doing this

Passwords alone stop very little. Phishing emails that copy the Thammasat sign-in page are the most common way student accounts get taken over, and a stolen password is enough on its own. With MFA switched on, the attacker still needs your phone, which they do not have. Ten minutes now saves a lot of trouble later.
