Buy a SIM at an operator's shop and take your passport. You do not need a Thai address or Thai ID.

## Buying and registering a SIM

Thai law requires every SIM to be registered. Since 18 August 2025 the registration includes a biometric check, which is a photo or short video of your face taken at the shop or in the operator's app. The check applies to a new SIM, a SIM swap and keeping a number.

- Buy from an operator's own shop, such as AIS or True, or an authorised dealer.
- Take your passport. Foreign nationals register with a passport.
- Ask the shop to register the SIM in your name before you leave.

Sources differ on newer limits, including a cap of three SIMs per operator and an activation deadline for new SIMs. Ask the shop for the current rule, or check [nbtc.go.th](https://www.nbtc.go.th).

Shops in the city are often cheaper than airport counters. Packages change often, so compare prices. See [what a month costs](/student-life/money/monthly-costs).

## Why you need a registered number

Bank apps, PromptPay and ride-hailing apps send codes to a Thai number. A tourist eSIM from a foreign provider may not work for these. Use a SIM registered in your own name. See [opening a bank account](/student-life/money/bank-account).

## Top up

Top up in the operator's app, at convenience stores or with a dial code. Note the date your monthly package renews. A shop can fix registration and activation problems. Take your passport and the SIM.

## TU Wi-Fi and eduroam

Sign in to TU Wi-Fi with your TU username and password. The university issues the account after you enrol. If the sign-in fails, follow the steps in [your first two weeks](/student-life/first-weeks/first-two-weeks) to reset the password.

eduroam is the academic network shared by universities. Connect to it with your TU account. The same account should work at other universities that use eduroam. Ask the TU ICT helpdesk on LINE at @icttuhelpdesk for the current settings.

Cafés, malls and most accommodation also have Wi-Fi.
