## The wai

The wai is a greeting and a sign of respect. You press your palms together at chest height and bow your head slightly. Students wai teachers and older people first. Return a wai when someone gives you one. If you are unsure, smile and nod. Do not wai children or people serving you, because they will not expect one.

Address teachers as Ajarn (อาจารย์) followed by their first name.

## Dress at temples and formal places

Cover your shoulders and knees at temples and many official buildings. Take your shoes off before you enter a temple hall or a home. A light scarf or sarong in your bag helps. The Grand Palace, near campus, refuses entry for sleeveless tops, short shorts and torn or tight clothing. Check the entry rules before you go.

## Respect for the monarchy and religion

Thai law protects the King, the Queen and the heir from criticism, and it applies to social media posts. Breaking it is a criminal offence, including for foreigners. Do not joke about the monarchy.

Do not touch Buddha images or climb on them. Women do not hand things to monks directly, and they do not touch them.

## Everyday manners

- If shoes are lined up by the door, add yours.
- Do not touch anyone's head. Do not point your feet at people or at Buddha images.
- Stay calm in a dispute. Raising your voice is seen as losing face.
- Tipping is not required. Leave small change in a restaurant if service was good.
- Keep noise down in your building at night.

## Buddhist holidays and opening hours

Major Buddhist holidays, Makha Bucha, Visakha Bucha, Asalha Bucha and Khao Phansa, fall on different dates each year because they follow the lunar calendar. Government offices and the university close on them. Some dates restrict alcohol sales, so check the current list. The rules have changed recently, and sources differ.

Check the TU academic calendar before you plan an errand at the Registrar's Office or the BIR office.

## Basic Thai phrases

Add ครับ (khrap) if you are a man, or ค่ะ (kha) if you are a woman, at the end of a phrase to be polite.
