## Documents

Branches set their own rules, so ask what the branch needs before you go. International students are usually asked for these documents.

- passport, original and a photocopy
- the visa page showing Non-Immigrant ED status
- an enrolment letter from the BIR office
- proof of address in Thailand, such as a TM30 receipt, a lease or a letter from your dorm

Banks generally do not open accounts for people who entered on a tourist or visa-exempt stay. Ask your landlord to file a TM30 for you. See [Visa rules while you study](/student-life/rules-and-rights/visa-rules).

## Bangkok Bank and TU Greats

Thammasat has a partnership with Bangkok Bank through the TU Greats app. The student card is also a Bangkok Bank card, and tuition refunds are paid to the linked account once it is activated. Ask the BIR office how to activate it. See [Tuition, fees and refunds](/student-life/money/tuition-and-fees).

## PromptPay

PromptPay is the Thai instant payment system. Link it to your mobile phone number in your bank's app, then send money or pay by scanning a QR code. You need a Thai number registered in your name for this. See [SIM cards and Wi-Fi](/student-life/first-weeks/sim-and-wifi).

## Money from abroad

Ask your home bank what it charges for an international transfer. Ask your Thai bank for its SWIFT code and account details. Foreign cards usually carry a flat fee for each ATM withdrawal, so check with your home bank.

## Scams

Install your bank's app only from the official app store. A bank or the university will never ask for the code sent to your phone. If a card is lost or an account is blocked, call the bank number printed on the card or visit a branch.
