Classes are only part of student life. The friendships, skills and memories that stick tend to come from what you do alongside your degree, and BIR has plenty of ways in.

## Clubs

Clubs at Thammasat run at several levels: within the BIR programme itself, across the whole of Tha Prachan Campus, and university-wide. You can join clubs at any (or all) of these levels; there's no rule that BIR students stick to BIR clubs only.

### BIR clubs

These are student clubs run by and for BIR students. Twelve of them cover a lot of ground: volunteer camps, a podcast, a writers' club, music, four sports teams including esports, a card game club, and three clubs that simulate an institution (the United Nations, the Thai Parliament, and an investment fund). Joining one is one of the fastest ways to meet people outside your immediate class group. See [Clubs](/clubs) for the full BIR club directory.

Thammasat also runs many university-wide clubs and societies, covering everything from academics to hobbies to service. As a Thammasat student, you can join those too.

## BIRSA events

BIRSA organises events throughout the year, orientation activities, socials, talks, and faculty-wide gatherings. These are open to all BIR students and are usually free or low-cost. Check [What's on](/news) regularly so you do not miss sign-up windows.

## Volunteering

Volunteering (whether through BIRSA, the faculty, or independent causes) is a good way to build experience while giving something back. Opportunities range from one-off event support to ongoing commitments. Keep an eye on BIRSA's channels for calls for volunteers, especially around bigger events.

## Why it's worth your time

- **Friendships that outlast a single semester** usually come from repeated, low-stakes contact; a weekly club meeting does more for this than occasional big events.
- **Skills that do not show up on a transcript** (organising, public speaking, teamwork) are built through clubs and volunteering more than through coursework alone.
- **A sense of belonging** matters for getting through harder weeks of the degree, and it's much easier to build before you need it than during a crisis.

## Getting started

If you're not sure where to begin, start small: turn up to one BIRSA event, or message a club you're curious about. Nobody expects a large commitment on day one; most people found their community by simply showing up once and returning because they enjoyed it.
