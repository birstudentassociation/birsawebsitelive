Looking after your health — physical and mental — is part of managing student life, not a separate emergency-only concern. This page covers where to turn, from a minor cold to something more serious.

## Everyday health care

Thammasat University runs health services for students, generally covering routine consultations, basic treatment and referrals to hospital when needed. If you're unwell, that's usually the first place to check rather than going straight to a private hospital.

## Mental health and counselling

Feeling overwhelmed, anxious, or low at some point during your degree is common — it doesn't mean something is wrong with you, and it isn't a weakness to ask for support. Thammasat provides counselling services for students, and starting the conversation is often the hardest part, not the actual appointment.

If you're supporting a friend who seems to be struggling, encouraging them to talk to someone — a counsellor, a trusted staff member, or even just you listening without judgement — can make a real difference.

## When to see a doctor versus wait it out

Minor colds and aches usually resolve with rest. See a doctor promptly if symptoms are severe, don't improve after a few days, or if you're ever unsure — it's always fine to get checked rather than guess.

## Taking care of yourself day to day

- Sleep and regular meals do more for mood and focus than most students expect during busy weeks.
- BIRSA and faculty events are also a low-pressure way to stay socially connected, which matters for wellbeing too — see [Getting involved](/student-life/home/getting-involved).
- If cost is a barrier to getting help, ask the health service directly about options rather than avoiding care altogether.

## In a crisis

If you or someone you know is in immediate danger, call 1669 or 191 without delay. Reaching out early — to a friend, a counsellor, or a helpline — is always the right call, not an overreaction.
