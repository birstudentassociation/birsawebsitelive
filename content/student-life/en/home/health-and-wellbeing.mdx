Looking after your health, physical and mental, is part of managing student life, not a separate emergency-only concern. This page covers where to turn, from a minor cold to something more serious.

## Everyday health care: the TU Virtual Clinic

The **TU Virtual Clinic** is the campus sick bay, in the health area beneath the student activity building, opposite the gym. It is open Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 16:30. Bring your student card and your ID card (or passport, for international students).

For most minor illnesses and injuries, this is the first place to check rather than going straight to a private hospital. If your symptoms are more serious than the clinic can handle, staff there can refer you on to hospital.

## What your student status pays for

As a Thammasat student, you are covered for both routine treatment and accidents. Knowing what is covered, and where, saves you money and time when something happens.

### Thammasat student healthcare entitlement

Treatment at Thammasat University Hospital (โรงพยาบาลธรรมศาสตร์เฉลิมพระเกียรติ) is covered as follows:

| Cover                                                                    | Amount                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| General treatment                                                        | 5,000 baht per visit, up to 20,000 baht per academic year |
| Basic dental (check up, filling, extraction, scaling, X-ray)             | Up to 300 baht per visit, 3 times per academic year       |
| Basic lab tests (CBC, stool exam, urine exam, plain film X-ray)          | Covered                                                   |
| Drugs on the national essential medicines list, room, and standard meals | Covered                                                   |

Note that Thammasat University Hospital is at the Rangsit campus, so this entitlement is worth planning around rather than relying on for something urgent at Tha Prachan.

Separately, if you hold the universal coverage "gold card" (บัตรทอง), the handbook sets out what it covers once you have transferred it to Thammasat University Hospital. Whether or not you transfer it, the gold card covers diagnosis, treatment and rehabilitation; food and a standard room as an inpatient; drugs and medical supplies on the national essential list; unlimited extractions, fillings and scaling; plastic-base dentures; and health promotion and disease prevention and control. In an accident or emergency you can use any hospital near where it happened, with no limit on which one.

### Accident insurance

Every Thammasat student is covered by student accident insurance. You can check your own cover by showing your ID card and student card to hospital staff.

If you are on the policy at a **contract hospital**, you can direct claim with no need to pay upfront. If you are not yet on the policy, you can be added afterwards. If you are on the policy but use a hospital that is not a contract hospital, you pay upfront yourself and then bring the documents to the Student Affairs office at your centre, who forward them to the insurance company for reimbursement.

The contract hospital for Tha Prachan is **Chao Phraya Pinklao Hospital** (โรงพยาบาลเจ้าพระยาปิ่นเกล้า).

| Cover                                                                                               | Amount       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| Accident treatment (per accident)                                                                   | 15,000 baht  |
| Death, loss of organ/sight/hearing/speech, or permanent disability from accident, assault or murder | 150,000 baht |
| Funeral costs, death from general illness                                                           | 15,000 baht  |

## Mental health and counselling

Feeling overwhelmed, anxious, or low at some point during your degree is common; it does not mean something is wrong with you, and it is not a weakness to ask for support. There are several real routes into support at Tha Prachan, so you know exactly what to do next.

- **TU Well Being, via the TU Greats App.** Book through the app. Online sessions run Monday to Friday; onsite sessions run Wednesday to Friday, 08:00 to 16:00. Onsite appointments happen on floor 3 of the Student Affairs office, in the student activity building. There is also a 24-hour booking line: 02-026-2345, press 2.
- **Viva City counselling centre.** Weekdays, 08:30 to 16:30, also bookable through the TU Greats App.
- **TCAPS**, the counselling centre at the Faculty of Liberal Arts, at both Tha Prachan and Rangsit.
- **Relationflip**, an online counselling service at [happy.relationflip.com/registerUniversity](https://happy.relationflip.com/registerUniversity). It does not prescribe medication. If you register but do not receive your login details by email, or run into any other issue, contact the RF call centre on 099-002-6888.

If you're supporting a friend who seems to be struggling, encouraging them to talk to someone (a counsellor, a trusted staff member, or even just you listening without judgement) can make a real difference.

## When to see a doctor versus wait it out

Minor colds and aches usually resolve with rest. See a doctor promptly if symptoms are severe, do not improve after a few days, or if you're ever unsure; it's always fine to get checked rather than guess. The TU Virtual Clinic is the easiest first stop.

## Taking care of yourself day to day

- Sleep and regular meals do more for mood and focus than most students expect during busy weeks.
- BIRSA and faculty events are also a low-pressure way to stay socially connected, which matters for wellbeing too. See [Getting involved](/student-life/home/getting-involved).
- If cost is a barrier to getting help, check what your student healthcare entitlement or accident insurance already covers before paying out of pocket.

## In a crisis

If you or someone you know is in immediate danger, call 1669 or 191 without delay. Reaching out early (to a friend, a counsellor, or a helpline) is always the right call, not an overreaction.
