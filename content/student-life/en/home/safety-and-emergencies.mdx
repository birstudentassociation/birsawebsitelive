Tha Prachan is generally a safe campus, but a little awareness goes a long way, especially around the river, at night, and with common scams targeting students.

## Campus security

Security staff are present around campus buildings and gates. If something feels wrong (an unfamiliar person loitering, a broken lock, a lighting issue), tell a guard or staff member rather than assuming someone else will.

## River safety

The Chao Phraya is a working river with strong currents and heavy boat traffic. A few basics:

- Wait for boats to fully dock before stepping on or off.
- Keep bags and phones held securely near the open sides of express boats.
- Avoid piers and railings late at night when lighting is poor.

## Common scams to know

Bangkok's tourist-facing areas near campus occasionally attract scams aimed at both visitors and students. Typical patterns include unofficial "closed today, but I can take you somewhere better" approaches near the Grand Palace, and unsolicited "great deal" offers from strangers. Politely declining and walking on is always fine; you don't owe anyone an explanation.

## Harassment and reporting

If you experience harassment (from another student, staff member, or member of the public), you have the right to report it and be taken seriously. Options include:

- Speaking to a trusted BIRSA committee member, who can help you understand next steps.
- Reporting to the faculty office or university's relevant welfare channel.
- Contacting BIRSA through [Contact BIRSA](/contact) if you're unsure where else to start; we can help point you the right way.

You don't have to already have "proof" or a fully formed complaint to start this conversation. Being unsure is a normal reason to reach out.

## Everyday precautions

- Share your live location with a friend when travelling alone late at night.
- Keep a photo of your student ID and important documents saved on your phone in case of loss or theft.
- Save campus security and a trusted contact's number somewhere you can reach quickly.

## In an emergency

For anything urgent and safety-related, call 1669 (medical) or 191 (police) first, then let BIRSA or the faculty know afterwards if it affects your studies or wellbeing.
