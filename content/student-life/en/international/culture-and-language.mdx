You do not need to master Thai culture before you arrive; most people appreciate visible effort far more than perfection. This page covers the basics that come up often.

## The wai

The wai, pressing your palms together at chest height with a slight bow, is Thailand's traditional greeting and sign of respect. As a student, you'll generally see it used toward teachers and elders. You're not expected to initiate it constantly, but returning a wai when offered one is polite, and a smile plus a slight nod is always a safe fallback if you're unsure.

## Dress codes at temples and formal spaces

Many temples and some official buildings expect modest dress: shoulders and knees covered, and shoes removed before entering certain areas. Carrying a light scarf or sarong in your bag is a simple way to be prepared without planning your whole outfit around it. The Grand Palace, near campus, enforces this strictly and can refuse entry for inappropriate dress.

## Buddhist holidays and opening hours

Thailand observes several Buddhist holidays across the year (such as Makha Bucha, Visakha Bucha, and Asalha Bucha), when some shops, government offices, and, on certain dates, alcohol sales may be restricted or closed. University schedules generally account for major public holidays, but it's worth checking the calendar before planning errands or travel around these dates.

## Everyday etiquette worth knowing

- Remove shoes when a home, some shops, and certain temple buildings clearly expect it; look for a pile of shoes at the entrance as a cue.
- Avoid touching people's heads or pointing your feet directly at people or Buddha images, since both are considered disrespectful.
- Public displays of anger or raised voices are viewed less favourably than in some other cultures; a calm, polite approach generally gets better results even when frustrated.

## If you get something wrong

Cultural missteps happen to almost everyone early on, and Thai people are generally forgiving of genuine visitors making an effort. A sincere apology and a willingness to learn go a long way.
