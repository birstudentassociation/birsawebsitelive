Thailand's healthcare system includes excellent hospitals in Bangkok, and Tha Prachan is close to some of the best-known. Here's what to know before you need them.

## Hospitals near campus

**Siriraj Hospital**, one of Thailand's largest and most respected hospitals, is directly across the river from Tha Prachan — reachable by a short cross-river boat. Several other hospitals and clinics, both public and private, serve the wider old-city area. Private hospitals tend to have shorter waits and more English-speaking staff, generally at higher cost than public hospitals.

## Insurance expectations

Many universities, including Thammasat, expect or require international students to hold valid health insurance for the duration of their stay — sometimes as a visa or enrolment condition. Confirm the specific requirement and any university-arranged options with TU International Affairs, since policies and requirements can change. Keep your insurance documents (physical or digital) accessible in case a hospital asks at check-in.

For non-urgent care, it's usually faster and cheaper to go directly to a hospital's outpatient department than to call an ambulance.

## Pharmacies

Pharmacies are common across Bangkok, easily identified by a green cross sign, and pharmacists can often help directly with minor ailments without needing a doctor's visit. Bring the name of your regular medication (or its active ingredient) if you take something specific, since brand names differ from your home country.

## Registering with a health provider

Some students choose a regular clinic or hospital near where they live for routine care, rather than deciding only when something goes wrong. It's worth doing this in your first few weeks rather than waiting for an emergency to figure out where to go.

## If you're ever unsure

When in doubt about how serious something is, err on the side of getting checked — Thai hospitals are used to treating international patients, and language barriers are rarely as large an obstacle as people expect.
