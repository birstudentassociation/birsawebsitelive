Your first week sets the tone for the rest of the semester. Here's a practical route through it, written by students who've done the same trip.

## From the airport to Tha Prachan

Bangkok has two main airports — Suvarnabhumi (BKK) and Don Mueang (DMK). From either, you have a few reliable options into the city:

- **Airport rail link + connections** — fast and predictable from Suvarnabhumi, though you'll need to connect to reach the old city near Tha Prachan.
- **Taxi or ride-hailing app** — simplest with luggage, especially on arrival. Agree to use the meter (taxis) or confirm the fare in-app before starting.
- **Pre-arranged pickup** — if your accommodation or a senior student can arrange this, it removes a lot of first-day stress.

Tha Prachan itself sits in the old city, near the river — tell your driver "Thammasat, Tha Prachan campus" rather than just "Thammasat," which has multiple campuses.

## What to set up first

In roughly this order:

1. Confirm your accommodation and get the keys/access sorted.
2. Get a local SIM card so you have data and a Thai number (see [Phones and internet](/student-life/international/phones-and-internet)).
3. Register with TU International Affairs and complete any arrival paperwork they require.
4. Open a Thai bank account once you have the necessary documents (see [Banking and money](/student-life/international/banking-and-money)).
5. Walk the route from your accommodation to campus at least once before your first class.
6. Save key numbers: campus security, your programme office, and BIRSA's contact.

## Your first week checklist

- [ ] Attend orientation sessions run by TU International Affairs and/or BIR.
- [ ] Locate your faculty building and where your classes are held.
- [ ] Meet your academic advisor or programme coordinator if introduced.
- [ ] Join BIRSA's channels for events and updates.
- [ ] Identify the nearest pharmacy, hospital, and grocery store to where you live.
- [ ] Note your visa's 90-day reporting deadline (see [Visa and immigration](/student-life/international/visa-and-immigration)).

## Settling in socially

BIRSA runs welcome events aimed specifically at helping new students meet each other — international and home students alike. These are worth prioritising even if you're jet-lagged; the first few connections you make often shape your whole first semester. Check [What's on](/news) for upcoming dates.

## If something goes wrong in week one

Don't try to solve everything alone. TU International Affairs handles visa and enrolment issues; BIRSA can help you find your feet socially and point you to the right office. See [Contact BIRSA](/services/contact) if you're not sure who to ask.
