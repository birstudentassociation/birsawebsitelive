Handling money in Thailand is straightforward once you have a local bank account set up, and most day-to-day payments run through it, directly or via linked apps.

## Opening a Thai bank account

Requirements vary by bank and sometimes by branch, but international students are typically asked for a combination of:

- Passport (original, plus a photocopy).
- Visa page showing your Non-Immigrant ED status.
- A letter confirming enrolment from the university or faculty.
- Proof of address in Thailand (sometimes a dorm or landlord letter).

Some banks are more accustomed to processing student applications than others, so ask other international students or TU International Affairs which branches near Tha Prachan tend to be smoothest for this.

## PromptPay

PromptPay is Thailand's national instant-payment system, linked to your phone number or national/passport ID. Once your account supports it, you can send and receive money instantly between banks, split bills with friends, and pay many small vendors by scanning a QR code. It's worth setting up early, since cash-only situations become rarer once you're used to PromptPay.

## Everyday spending

- Bank cards are widely accepted in shops, but smaller stalls and markets are often cash or QR-code only.
- Mobile banking apps let you check balances, transfer money, and pay bills without visiting a branch.
- Keep a small amount of cash on hand for markets, food stalls, and transport.

## Sending or receiving money from home

International transfers usually work through your home bank or a transfer service, arriving into your Thai account. Fees and exchange rates vary between providers, so comparing a couple of options before a large transfer is generally worthwhile.

## If you run into account issues

Lost cards, blocked accounts, or login problems are usually resolved fastest through your bank's official app or branch rather than a Google search, since Thai banking apps typically support English. If a document you're missing is blocking account opening, TU International Affairs can sometimes help clarify what the university can issue on your behalf.
