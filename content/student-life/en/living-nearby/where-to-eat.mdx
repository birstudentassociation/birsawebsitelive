## Cheap food near campus

- The canteens in the faculties and around the university are the cheapest option. They have rice and curry stalls, noodle stands and drinks counters.
- Small family-run shops on the streets around Tha Prachan sell everything from boat noodles to grilled skewers.
- Wang Lang (Siriraj) market is across the river, reached by ferry from Tha Prachan. It is busiest in the evening.

Market stalls mostly take cash, so carry small notes and coins.

At New Yong Hua Phochana, order the duck noodles, duck rice or red pork rice. Each bowl comes with soup.

หม่าล่าหน้ามอ has several dipping sauces, a choice of spice level and a sweet sauce for people who cannot eat spicy food.

ป้าป้อมขนมโตเกียว has savoury and sweet fillings.

แซ่บอำหลำ serves Isaan food.

The juice stall adjusts the sweetness on request.

LUA Café and Chang Khua give 10 percent off with a student card. See [Student discounts](/student-life/money/student-discounts).

## Halal and vegetarian food

Many stalls use pork, and many dishes contain fish sauce, so ask before you order.

- Say ฮาลาล (halal) to ask for halal food. The map has a halal category. Aheesah Roddee Halal (อาอีซะฮ์ รสดี) serves halal biryani and satay.
- Say เจ (jay) to ask for vegetarian food. Jay food has no meat, fish or fish sauce and often no garlic or onion. Stalls fly a yellow flag with เจ during the Vegetarian Festival, held in September or October.
- Say ไม่ใส่หมู (mai sai mu) to ask for no pork.

## Eating for less

- Rice with a single topping (ข้าวราดแกงหน้าเดียว) is usually the cheapest hot meal.
- Many buildings have free drinking water points.
- Convenience stores cost more than market stalls for a full meal.
- Most dorms near Tha Prachan do not allow cooking. Some have a shared kitchen. Ask before you rent. See [Finding somewhere to live](/student-life/living-nearby/housing).

The Office of International Affairs estimates meals at 8,000 baht a month. See [What a month costs](/student-life/money/monthly-costs).
