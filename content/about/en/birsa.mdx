BIRSA — the BIR Student Association — is the student-run association for students of the Bachelor of Political Science in Politics and International Relations (BIR) at Thammasat University's Faculty of Political Science.

## Our mission

We exist to make student life at BIR better — socially, practically, and in how students are represented. That means running events, maintaining this portal as a clear source of information, and giving students a voice in matters that affect their time at BIR.

BIRSA is student-run and unofficial: we work alongside the BIR programme and faculty, but we are not a university office. For official academic and enrolment matters, always use the BIR Program's own channels (see [Contact](/about/contact)).

The committee is organised around a small set of roles, each responsible for a different part of what BIRSA does:

- **President** — overall leadership and representation of BIRSA to the faculty and university.
- **Vice President** — supports the President and typically leads on internal coordination.
- **Secretary** — records decisions, manages communication and documentation.
- **Treasurer** — manages BIRSA's finances and event budgets.
- **Head of Student Welfare** — leads on student support, wellbeing initiatives, and this portal's student-life guide.
- **Head of Activities** — plans and runs events, from orientation to socials.
- **Head of Communications** — runs BIRSA's socials, news updates, and outward-facing messaging.

## How decisions are made

Committee decisions are generally made collectively, with members discussing proposals relevant to their area before larger decisions are agreed as a group. Bigger initiatives — new events, major changes to student services — typically involve wider consultation with BIR students, not just the committee alone.

## How to join the committee

Committee positions typically open up around the start of each academic year, with an announcement made through BIRSA's social channels and [What's on](/news). If you're interested in getting involved before a formal opening, reaching out to a current committee member is a reasonable first step — see [Contact BIRSA](/services/contact).

You don't need to be an existing committee member to contribute — many BIRSA activities also rely on volunteers and short-term helpers around specific events. See [Getting involved](/student-life/home/getting-involved) for more ways in.
