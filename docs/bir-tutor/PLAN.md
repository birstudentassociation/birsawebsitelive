# Build BIR Tutor on retrieval, rubrics and people

The best-evidenced pedagogy a website can deliver is not a chatbot. It is a well-sequenced set of plain techniques: **frequent low-stakes retrieval with corrective feedback** (g ≈ 0.50, and about 0.73 when feedback is given), **spacing timed to the exam date**, **worked and annotated examples that fade into independent work**, **explicit writing-strategy instruction with a single rubric**, **focused rather than blanket error correction**, and **structured human tutoring delivered in intensive blocks**. For BIR Tutor this means two different products with two different honesty problems. In Round 1 (Inter-Portfolio 1), the BIR office ranks applicants on an SAT Evidence-Based Reading and Writing score that is **already fixed for this cycle**: applications close at 23:59 on Friday 6 November 2026 and the next SAT sitting is 7 November. The only lever left is the committee interview on Sunday 15 November, so the Round 1 track is a four-week interview-rehearsal service and nothing else. In Round 3 (TCAS 3), the written exam on Sunday 21 March 2027 is 100% of the selection score (40% politics and international relations multiple choice, 60% English essay), which leaves a 22-week window in which retrieval practice, spacing and strategy-based writing instruction can each do real work. Generative AI earns a narrow, guarded role: evidence shows answer-giving AI **lowered later unassisted exam scores by 17%**, while the strongest positive results came from AI that was grounded in expert content, structured, and supervised by humans. BIRSA can ship a credible Round 1 service by about 1 November with static pages, in-browser recording and volunteer mock interviews, and a fuller Round 3 platform in stages by February, provided it keeps the service free and open to all, keeps current panel members out of tutoring, uses no insider exam content, and treats applicants' essays and voices as minors' personal data under the PDPA.

## Six techniques survive scrutiny, and several popular ones do not

### Retrieval, spacing and feedback form the core

Practice testing is the best-supported technique for self-study. A classroom-only meta-analysis of **222 studies and 48,478 students** found an overall quizzing effect of **g = 0.499**, larger with corrective feedback, repeated tests and practice that matches the final test format ([Yang et al. 2021](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/testing-quizzing-boosts-classroom-learning-a-systematic-and-meta-/)). Rowland's mostly laboratory synthesis gives a similar g = 0.50, and secondary summaries of that paper report **g = 0.73 with feedback against 0.39 without** ([Rowland 2014](https://courseware.epfl.ch/assets/courseware/v1/fdde2f0aa590bf3b1324077a6bf1540c/asset-v1%3AEPFL%2BDEMO%2B2020%2Btype%40asset%2Bblock/Rowland2014-meta-analysis.pdf); [secondary figures](https://studycardsai.com/blog/active-recall-statistics)). That contrast is the largest single design lever available to a question bank. Practice tests also beat every other comparison condition in a third synthesis of 272 effect sizes ([Adesope et al. 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654316689306)). One unresolved conflict deserves honesty: some secondary accounts of Adesope report feedback as non-significant, which sits awkwardly beside Rowland, so the safe design is to give feedback anyway, since it is cheap and nothing suggests it harms retrieval.

Spacing carries the largest laboratory effect. In an online study of 1,354 adults, the best gap between study sessions produced **a 64% increase in final recall (d = 1.1)** over no gap, and the optimal gap shrank from **about 20–40% of the time to the test at one week to 5–10% at one year**; gaps that were too long cost far less than gaps that were too short ([Cepeda et al. 2008](https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaVulRohrerWixtedPashler-PS-2008.pdf)). This is factual recall by adults, not essay writing by 17-year-olds, so it supports scheduling the multiple-choice knowledge and interview evidence bank, not the essay itself. Applied to BIR's calendar, a Round 1 applicant 27 days from interview should revisit each prepared answer about five to seven days after first working on it, and a Round 3 applicant with about 150 days to go should revisit each knowledge unit every two to three weeks, tightening as March approaches. Interleaving gives a moderate g = 0.42 overall, but **g = −0.39 for word lists**, where blocking is better, and a non-significant effect for expository texts ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)). BIR Tutor should therefore interleave only where the hard part is choosing an approach, such as mixed interview question families or mixed multiple-choice topics after each topic has first been taught on its own.

Feedback needs designing, not just switching on. The most recent synthesis puts its average effect at **d = 0.48**, well below the 0.7–0.79 once claimed, with large variation depending on what the feedback contains ([Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087/full)). The older Kluger and DeNisi meta-analysis found that feedback **reduced performance in over a third of cases**, mostly when it pulled attention toward the person rather than the task ([Kluger & DeNisi 1996](https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Marking%20and%20Feedback/The%20effects%20of%20feedback%20interventions.pdf)). Every response BIR Tutor gives should therefore say what is right, why, and what to do next, and should avoid praise of the person, leaderboards and comparisons with other applicants.

### Examples, strategies and rubrics carry the essay

Worked examples help novices, with **g = 0.48** in mathematics, and studies that added self-explanation prompts escaped the negative results seen without them ([Barbieri, Clerjuste & Chawla 2023](https://oaks.kent.edu/hcri/meta-analysis-worked-examples-effect-mathematics-performance)). The benefit reverses as learners gain expertise ([Kalyuga 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs10648-007-9054-3)), so examples must fade. For essays, the analogue is an annotated model essay labelled against the marking criteria, followed by partial tasks and then whole essays; that transfer is an inference, because no meta-analysis isolates annotated model essays. Writing research actually ranks the study of models low. Writing Next, a synthesis of 142 studies for grades 4 to 12, puts **explicit strategy instruction for planning and revising first** of eleven elements, followed by summarisation, collaborative writing and specific product goals, with the study of models near the bottom ([AdLit summary of Writing Next](https://www.adlit.org/topics/writing/summary-writing-next)). A secondary summary puts strategy instruction at about 0.82 ([hickstro.org](https://hickstro.org/2007/06/)), and Self-Regulated Strategy Development (SRSD), which adds goal-setting and self-monitoring to the strategy, reached an average of 1.33 in learners with learning disabilities ([Texas Center for Learning Disabilities](https://texasldcenter.org/?p=338)). No SRSD meta-analysis covers adult or EFL writers, so applying it to Thai applicants is extrapolation, though from the strongest base available.

Rubrics have a moderate effect on performance (**g = 0.45**) ([Panadero et al. 2023](https://deustoteka.deusto.es/items/59022074-df96-4c6a-8c52-3aa513c0ae3e)), and self-assessment reliably improves self-regulation (0.23–0.65) and self-efficacy (0.73) ([Panadero, Jonsson & Botella 2017](https://portalinvestigacion.deusto.es/documentos/6326545dd50fae52cd317f7b)). For error correction, written corrective feedback improves L2 accuracy with a **small-to-moderate** effect, and focused feedback on a few error types tends to beat marking everything ([Journal of Language and Education, citing Kang & Han 2015 and Kao & Wible 2014](https://jle.hse.ru/article/view/16516)). Thai studies agree on what to focus on: **plural nouns, articles (especially a missing "the"), subject–verb agreement, punctuation, fragments and run-ons** ([rEFLections](https://so05.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/reflections/article/download/248862/169222/872512); [ELT journal 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ibn/eltjnl/v10y2017i3p101.html)), plus topic–comment "double subject" sentences carried over from Thai ([JLA](https://so07.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JLA/article/view/10848)), with agreement errors most frequent in argumentative essays ([MPJH](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/mpjh/article/view/268155)).

### People still beat software, if they are structured

Tutoring is among the best-evidenced interventions, with a pooled effect of **0.29–0.37 SD**, stronger with trained tutors ([Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476); [Stanford NSSA](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/promise-tutoring-prek-12-learning-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-experimental-evidence)). The volunteer evidence splits sharply on dosage. Italy's Tutoring Online Program, in which university volunteers gave **three hours a week of one-to-one** online tutoring, raised maths by 0.20–0.22 SD ([AEA RCT Registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/5607)), while a low-dosage US college-volunteer pilot produced **0.04–0.07 SD, not significant** ([Kraft et al. 2022](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/pandp.20221038)). Software tutoring that checks each step (0.76) roughly matches human tutors (0.79), whereas systems that only mark final answers reach 0.31 ([VanLehn 2011](https://quality.mit.edu/files/2012/01/Quality-Symposium-Kurt-VanLehn.pdf)). Gains on locally written tests also run much larger than on standardised ones ([Kulik & Fletcher 2016](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1030353.pdf)), a warning for how BIR Tutor should read its own quiz data.

Planning helps when it is concrete. "If it is 7 pm on Tuesday, I do 20 minutes of questions" style implementation intentions show **d ≈ 0.65** on goal attainment in secondary summaries ([Frontiers, citing Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/pdf)). Nudges do not rescue a self-paced service: planning prompts tested on about 250,000 MOOC learners raised early engagement but **not completion**, and effects shrank about tenfold from pilot to scale ([Kizilcec et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7334459)). BIR applicants have a hard external deadline that MOOC learners lack, which is the service's best defence against drop-off.

### What the evidence tells BIRSA not to build

Rereading, highlighting and summarising-as-review are rated low utility ([Dunlosky et al., via APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)), so no "review notes" mode should end without a retrieval check. Growth-mindset messages have **weak overall effects** limited to lower achievers ([Sisk et al. 2018](<https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2018/Sisk,%20Burgoyne%20et%20al.%20(2018)%20-%20Mindset%20and%20Academic%20Achievement.pdf>)), and values affirmation failed a well-powered replication that ruled out effects above 0.10 ([Hanselman et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5403146)). Interview coaching itself has a thin record: coached and uncoached medical applicants showed **no difference in multiple mini-interview scores** or entry rates ([MJA 2015](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2015/203/9/reducing-impact-coaching-selection-medicine)), though repeat exposure to similar stations did help ([MJA 2014](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2014/201/7/multiple-mini-interview-performance-repeat-applicants-medical-school-admission)). The defensible Round 1 aim is therefore familiarity, organised answers and lower anxiety, not scripted answers. Second-language anxiety correlates **r = −0.36** with achievement across 19,933 learners ([Teimouri, Goetze & Plonsky 2019](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/second-language-anxiety-and-achievement/)), and a short stress-reappraisal module has the best recent evidence of the anxiety interventions, from small and mid-sized trials ([Harvard DASH, Jamieson et al.](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/4214916); [NICHD on Yeager et al. 2022](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/newsroom/news/070622-teen-stress)).

## Two cohorts face two different problems

The users are Thai upper-secondary students and recent leavers, mostly **aged 17–19 and therefore minors under Thai law** ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-issues-guidelines-on-pdpa-consent-and-notification-requirements/2/)), applying to an English-medium programme. Round 1 applicants tend to come from schools where the SAT is familiar; Round 3 is open to Thai applicants only and accepts IELTS, TOEFL, TU-GET or SAT results, so it draws a broader field. Paid SAT, IELTS and interview coaching is concentrated in Bangkok and online one-to-one formats, and participation in tutoring tracks family socio-economic status ([Journal of International Studies](https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jis/article/view/247257)). The 2027-intake reforms make central exams free for up to seven subjects and waive Round 3 application fees for up to seven choices ([Honeykids Asia](https://honeykidsasia.com/thailand/tcas70-thailand-university-admissions-update/)), which shifts the remaining cost barrier onto preparation. That residual gap is BIR Tutor's reason to exist. Rural Thai students scored **53–70 PISA points** below urban peers in 2022 ([Thairath English](https://en.thairath.co.th/scoop/theissue/2908035)); BIR applicants are a selected group, so this justifies outreach to provincial schools rather than describing the typical applicant.

According to the BIR office's official 2027 announcement as summarised on the BIRSA website (reviewed 17 October 2026), Round 1 offers 45 seats, ranks applicants by SAT EBRW with no minimum score, no minimum GPAX and no portfolio, and calls the top scorers to interview. The College Board calendar settles the score question: the 3 October 2026 sitting's results are already out, and **the 7 November sitting falls the day after applications close** ([College Board dates](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/dates-deadlines)). An applicant without a qualifying SAT cannot obtain one in time, and an applicant with one cannot change it. BIR Tutor must say this plainly on the first screen. SAT preparation belongs to next year's cohort, and even then controlled studies put coaching at roughly **6–9 points on the verbal section** ([Domingue & Briggs 2009](https://cepa.stanford.edu/content/using-linear-regression-and-propensity-score-matching-estimate-effect-coaching-sat)), with uncontrolled studies overstating effects four to five times ([Briggs](https://www.nepc.colorado.edu/sites/default/files/Briggs_SATCoachingBiasandCasualInference.pdf)); the widely quoted "20 hours on Khan Academy is worth 115 points" is an association, not a causal effect ([College Board newsroom](https://newsroom.collegeboard.org/new-data-links-20-hours-personalized-official-sat-practice-khan-academy-115-point-average-score)).

Round 1 applicants need four things in four weeks: an accurate picture of what the committee interview is and is not, organised answers to predictable question families, enough rehearsal under realistic pressure that the format holds no surprises, and, for the many who will not be called on 13 November, a clear bridge into Round 3. Round 3 applicants need a valid English test result before the February application window, a structured body of politics and international relations knowledge that they can retrieve under time pressure, a taught essay-writing routine that holds up in roughly 70 minutes (an inference from splitting a two-hour paper by weight, to confirm), targeted repair of their own recurring grammar errors, and a short interview refresher after scores arrive on 22 April. No public specification of the BIR general-knowledge paper was found ([TU Admissions](https://www.tuadmissions.in.th/img/2025112701490623.pdf)), so the knowledge bank must be built from a public core syllabus and labelled as practice, not as a prediction of the real paper.

## Round 1 track: four weeks of interview rehearsal to 15 November

The Round 1 track has one goal: applicants walk into the Tha Prachan interview having answered every common question family aloud at least three times, spaced about a week apart, and having faced at least one realistic mock with unseen follow-up questions. The committee interview's language and format are not stated in the summary; the plan assumes English with possible Thai, and this is the first question to confirm with the BIR office. Mock interviews are free and open to anyone who has applied or intends to apply, not only to those later called, because the call list arrives just two days before the interview. If demand exceeds volunteer capacity, each applicant gets an equal cap (two live mocks) and remaining slots go by lottery, which keeps access equal without collecting sensitive data on who needs help most.

## Round 3 track: 22 weeks to 21 March across knowledge, essay and interview

The Round 3 track runs three strands on one exam-date clock. The **knowledge strand** covers about 15 units of a public core syllabus (sovereignty and the state; realism, liberalism and constructivism; the security dilemma; international regimes; the UN system; ASEAN; regime types and democracy; the Thai constitution and institutions; major wars and treaties; globalisation and political economy; Thai foreign policy; current affairs), each taught briefly and then drilled through spaced retrieval with explained answers. The **essay strand** follows the SRSD sequence of building background knowledge, discussing and modelling a planning-and-revising strategy, memorising it, supported practice with fading scaffolds, then independent timed performance. The **interview strand** reuses the Round 1 kit after scores are released. Volunteers spend weeks 1–4 on Round 1 mocks, so the Round 3 track opens lightly and reaches full strength on 16 November.

## AI belongs behind the tutor, not in front of the applicant

The evidence on generative AI points one way. In a field experiment with nearly a thousand Turkish high-school students, unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice grades by 48% but left students **17% lower on a later unassisted exam**; a guardrailed tutor raised practice grades by 127% yet only matched the control on the exam ([Bastani et al., PNAS 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html)). Guardrails removed the harm without producing a gain. The strongest positive student-facing result, **0.63 SD** in a Harvard physics crossover trial, came from a tutor fed **pre-written expert solutions** and walked through a fixed problem sequence, with no delayed retention test and a disputed headline size ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/); [re-analysis](https://www.clayford.net/posts/ai_tutoring_nature/)). A teacher-supervised programme in Nigeria raised English by **0.23 SD**, still a working paper, and helped higher-baseline students most ([World Bank](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/15e1ff08-15ae-4f7a-b2a8-d146e6c113ee/full)). The design most relevant to BIRSA is Tutor CoPilot, where AI suggested moves to **human tutors** rather than to students: mastery rose 4 percentage points, and 9 for students of weaker tutors, while tutors gave away fewer answers ([Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017)). The engagement problem cuts the other way: in an 18-school trial of Khanmigo, most students stopped using it once it refused to give answers, and gains matched Khan Academy without AI ([FutureEd](https://www.future-ed.org/students-tried-khanmigos-ai-tutor-most-didnt-want-its-help/)). The most-cited meta-analysis claiming large ChatGPT benefits (g = 0.867) has been **retracted** ([GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/nature-retracts-oft-cited-paper-on-positive-impact-of-chatgpt)).

BIR Tutor's AI role should therefore follow a fixed order of priority. First, a **reviewer copilot** drafts rubric-referenced, focused feedback on an essay, which a trained BIR volunteer must edit and approve before the applicant sees it, the same pattern the course-review summariser already uses. Second, and only after that works, an **opt-in essay coach** sits inside the required faded-task flow rather than as a free chat box. It may ask planning questions, point to where an error type occurs and ask the applicant to fix it, and quote the relevant annotated model; it may not write, rewrite or complete sentences, paragraphs or outlines, and it reveals a correction only after the applicant has tried. It is grounded in BIRSA-authored rubric text, model essays and the knowledge organiser, not in open web content. It gives **no score or band prediction**, because LLM agreement with human raters on EFL argumentative essays is only fair ([IUC record](https://avesis.iuc.edu.tr/yayin/a433340f-6e81-4eb6-ba9f-f334fca4e436/chatgpt-4o-as-an-automated-scoring-tool-for-writing-assessment-strengths-and-weaknesses)), although low-stakes formative feedback is reasonable, given automated writing feedback's g = 0.55 ([Fleckenstein et al. 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10351274/)). BIRSA should **never run AI detectors** on applicants' writing: seven detectors falsely flagged non-native TOEFL essays **61% of the time on average**, and 97.8% were flagged by at least one ([Liang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02819)). For interviews, AI conversation practice reduces reported anxiety in small studies but showed no achievement difference ([Susoy 2026](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12832806/)), so a random prompt deck and timer deliver most of the value without AI speech scoring.

No student-facing AI should launch before 15 November. Before any launch, volunteers should red-team the coach for answer leakage, prompt injection from pasted text and age-inappropriate output, and the provider must offer no-training and minimal-retention terms, since every essay sent abroad is a cross-border transfer.

## Trained BIR students, kept at arm's length from selection

Volunteers should be current BIR students in years 2–4, recruited openly, and **excluded** if they sit on or assist interview panels, invigilate or mark the written exam, handle applications in the BIR office, or have seen exam content. Each signs a short conflict-of-interest declaration that also covers relatives or close friends among applicants and any paid tutoring work, and agrees not to promote commercial courses or exchange personal LINE contacts with applicants. Training comes in three short sessions: conflict of interest, safeguarding of minors and PDPA handling; rubric calibration on anchor scripts and recorded anchor answers until reviewers agree within one band; and scripted practice in running a mock and giving task- and process-level feedback that names no more than three error types. Peer tutoring works best with trained tutors in **intensive four- to ten-week blocks** ([Education Scotland mirror of the EEF toolkit](https://nrs.preservation.mirrorweb.com/public/20191211024427mp_/https://education.gov.scot/improvement/eef-toolkit/peer-tutoring/)), which matches the two blocks this plan uses. Volunteer hours, not software, set capacity, so the dosage evidence argues for fewer applicants receiving several sessions over many receiving one, within the equal-cap rule.

Conflict-of-interest safeguards go beyond tutor selection. Every page carries a bilingual statement that BIR Tutor is run by students, is independent of the admissions committee, has no access to exam questions, and that using it neither helps nor harms an application. All practice items are original or drawn from public sources; recollections of past papers from students who sat them are refused. The service is free with no selection of who may use it, BIRSA collects no data on who used it that could reach the admissions office, and BIRSA should seek a written acknowledgement from the Faculty, since no Thammasat or TCAS rule on student-run preparation was found ([TruePlookPanya](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/72105-blo-)).

## Equity and data design follow from who the users are

About **98% of Thai internet users browse on phones** and LINE reaches about 56 million users ([Meltwater](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/social-media-statistics-thailand)), so the default user is on a mid-range Android phone over mobile data. Pages stay light with no autoplay video, drafts and recordings save locally, every instruction appears in Thai while exam-language tasks stay in English, and audio has a text alternative. A LINE Official Account is the expected channel for reminders and links, but it should carry only links back to the site, never essays or recordings. Mock interviews default to audio, not video, to save data and reduce exposure, with evening and weekend slots. Outreach should target provincial schools directly. Thai typesetting needs care: `lang="th"` markup so browsers apply dictionary-based line breaking, and adequate size for Thai diacritics, consistent with the repository's own warning about Thai rewrapping.

Under the PDPA, PDPC guidelines treat 10–20-year-olds as a group for whom parental consent is not always required, but capacity rules make sole reliance on a minor's consent risky ([Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-issues-guidelines-on-pdpa-consent-and-notification-requirements/2/)), and transfers abroad need adequate protection, safeguards or informed consent under sections 28 and 29 ([DataGuidance](https://www.DataGuidance.com/opinion/thailand-operationalising-pdpa-data-transfers-and)). The data design therefore minimises at every step. Reading materials, the question bank and the recorder need no account. An optional account holds only a display name, an email address and an "I am under 20" flag that triggers a guardian acknowledgement; no national ID, school, grades or LINE ID. Interview recordings never leave the device unless the applicant chooses to share one with a volunteer for a specific session. Essays submitted for peer review are stored in Vercel Postgres, which itself may sit outside Thailand and must be disclosed as a cross-border transfer. AI processing of an essay needs a separate, explicit opt-in that names the provider and country. Everything is deleted by 30 June 2027, after Round 4 closes, or sooner on request through the existing rights-request route, and the notice is layered, bilingual and plain. Two questions need answers before launch: whether BIRSA or Thammasat is the data controller, and what the PDPC's October 2026 AI guidance (advisory) requires.

## Measure learning honestly and claim nothing about admission

The evidence warns that practice performance is not learning, in Bastani's trial most of all, and that gains on home-made tests exceed gains on real ones. BIR Tutor should measure what it can measure cleanly: dosage (questions answered, essays submitted, mocks attended), delayed retrieval accuracy on held-out items the applicant has not seen for at least two weeks, blind double-marked rubric scores on a diagnostic and a final timed essay with inter-rater agreement reported, the shrinking gap between self-assessed and reviewer scores, and self-reported anxiety before and after mocks. Feedback designs can be A/B tested inside the service, given that feedback harms in a third of comparisons. BIRSA should not request or link admissions records, and should not compare admission rates of users and non-users, because applicants who choose a free prep service differ from those who do not. It must not claim that BIR Tutor raises admission chances, cite "two sigma" style effects, or advertise score gains; a realistic aspiration for the strongest components is a few tenths of a standard deviation on its own measures.

## Conclusion

The research reframes BIR Tutor's job. The flashy component, an AI tutor, has the weakest and most double-edged evidence, while the components with the strongest evidence, retrieval with explained answers, schedules keyed to the real exam date, taught writing strategies with one rubric, and trained near-peer tutors, are cheap, buildable on the existing site and fully compatible with the PDPA and conflict-of-interest constraints. The constraint that matters most is not technical but temporal: for Round 1 the decisive variable was fixed before the service existed, and the most valuable thing BIRSA can do is say so and redirect effort to the interview and to Round 3.

A second implication is that BIRSA's advantage is people, not software. A pool of trained BIR students giving structured, calibrated feedback in two intensive blocks matches the design of the volunteer programmes that worked, and an AI copilot that improves those students' feedback, rather than replacing them, is the one AI configuration with RCT support. If the service measures delayed, unassisted performance and resists claiming admission effects, it will also produce something rare: honest evidence on whether near-peer, web-delivered preparation helps Thai applicants to an English-medium programme.
