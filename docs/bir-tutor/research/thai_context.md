# Thai Context for BIR Tutor (admissions, tutoring market, equity, peer tutoring evidence, PDPA, ethics, accessibility)

Researcher notes, compiled 19 Oct 2026. Budget-limited pass (about 16 tool calls). Many Thai sources were reached only through search-result summaries, not full-page fetches. These are marked **[snippet only]**. Thai-language-only sources are marked **[TH]**. Anything not confirmed in this session is under Gaps, not Findings.

## 1. TCAS structure for the 2027 intake (TCAS70) and how international programmes select

### Takeaway

TCAS70 (academic year 2570, entry 2027) keeps the four rounds, but it is a reform year. Central exams (TGAT/TPAT/A-Level) move earlier to January 2027 and become free for up to seven subjects. Round 3 (Admission) application fees are waived for up to seven choices. International programmes like BIR mostly run their own early "Inter" portfolio and written-exam tracks, which carry their own dates (e.g. BIR's 15 Nov 2026 interview and 21 Mar 2027 written exam). Those dates differ from the national Round 1 and Round 3 calendar and must be confirmed on tuadmissions.in.th.

### Cited Findings

- The 2027-intake TCAS overhaul was announced 26 April 2026 by the Minister of Higher Education (Prof. Dr. Yoschanan Wongswasdi) and the Council of University Presidents of Thailand chair (Prof. Dr. Wilert Puriwat). Key entrance exams become free for up to 7 subjects, and Round 3 (Admission) application fees are waived for up to 7 programme choices. — [Honeykids Asia, TCAS70 update](https://honeykidsasia.com/thailand/tcas70-thailand-university-admissions-update/) **[snippet only; page returned 403 on fetch]**
- Central exams moved to January 2027, earlier than before, partly to avoid seasonal flooding. Exams sit nationwide at the same time. — [Honeykids Asia](https://honeykidsasia.com/thailand/tcas70-thailand-university-admissions-update/) **[snippet only]**; [Bangkok Biznews, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/news/news-update/1231277) **[TH, snippet only]**
- TCAS70 timeline from Thai press: the myTCAS system opens 15 July 2026, and Portfolio (Round 1) applications through universities open 15 August 2026. Round 3 Admission applications on myTCAS run 7–11 May 2027, plus a 12–13 May window without the fee subsidy. Round 4 Direct Admission runs 29 May to 15 June 2027. — [The Standard, TCAS70](https://thestandard.co/tcas70-free-exam-folio-january/) **[TH, snippet only]**; [Daily News](https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/5813488/) **[TH, snippet only]**
- Round 1 Portfolio national result/confirmation dates were reported as 10 March 2027 (results), 10–11 March (confirm) and 12 March (waive). — [PPTV HD36 TCAS70 calendar](https://www.pptvhd36.com/news/%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A1/274111) **[TH, snippet only; not fully verified]**
- Student registration portal is student.mytcas.com. — [PSU Communication Sciences notice](https://www.commsci.psu.ac.th/?p=19413) **[TH, snippet only]**
- Example of a Round 2 Quota schedule: Mahidol ICT's quota round for academic year 2027 runs March–April 2027 with its own eligibility criteria. — [Mahidol ICT, Round 2 Quota](https://www.ict.mahidol.ac.th/en/round-2-quota-tcas-2-academic-year-2026/)
- Thammasat inter-programme precedent (Faculty of Law, Inter Portfolio). A 2023 TU admissions document lists SAT (or GSAT) as an option with minimum 550 Reading and Writing and 1,200 total. Scores go direct from College Board (TU code 7420). A 2024 document lists a 15-seat SAT-track quota in Inter Portfolio 2. — [tuadmissions.in.th PDF 2023](https://www.tuadmissions.in.th/img/2023111309200575.pdf); [tuadmissions.in.th PDF 2024](https://tuadmissions.in.th/img/2024121607221361.pdf) **[snippet only; Law faculty, not BIR]**
- TU international programmes accept a range of English tests for proficiency (TU-GET, IELTS, TOEFL, SAT, Duolingo, among others). This is shown for the Pridi Banomyong International College SPD programme in 2026. — [SPD TU apply page](https://spdtu.com/apply/) **[snippet only; SPD, not BIR]**

### Inferences

- The free-exam and fee-waiver reform lowers the _official_ cost barrier. The remaining cost barrier is preparation (SAT fees, tutoring, IELTS), so a free prep service targets the real residual gap.
- BIR's Round 1 interview (15 Nov 2026) comes well before the national Portfolio result date (10 Mar 2027). That is consistent with TU inter programmes announcing within the Round 1 window, but confirm against the BIR announcement. The service's content calendar should key off BIR's own dates, not the national calendar.
- The "one seat" principle (confirming in one round removes you from later rounds) is standard TCAS. Applicants who accept a Round 1 BIR seat would exit later rounds, so the service should explain this plainly. Not confirmed for TCAS70 in this session (see Gaps).

### Gaps

- Could not reach mytcas.com or tuadmissions.in.th directly for the official TCAS70 calendar or the BIR-specific 2570 announcement (SAT minimums, IELTS minimums, quotas, written-exam format). Report writer should treat BIR dates as given by the coordinator, not as verified here.
- One-seat/confirmation rules for TCAS70 not verified in a 2026 source.
- How Chula (e.g. BBA/EBA/BALAC) and Mahidol International College select (SAT/IELTS cut-offs, own exams such as MUIC's) was not researched in this pass.

## 2. Tutoring schools, prices, and the shadow-education market

### Takeaway

Reliable, current baht prices for SAT, IELTS or inter-programme interview prep were not found in this pass. Market-size figures for Thai shadow education are weak and undated (about 8 billion baht, unverified). The qualitative evidence consistently links tutoring participation to family socio-economic status, and links demand to the entrance-exam system and gaps in school provision.

### Cited Findings

- Thai private tutoring is "valued at around 8 billion baht", attributed to Longtunman.com. The article is undated and the figure is unverified. — [Chiang Mai CityLife, Thailand's Shadow Education](https://www.chiangmaicitylife.com/citylife-articles/thailands-shadow-education-2/)
- TDRI researcher Nicha Piyapongsakorn: "The demand for private tutoring is caused by inadequate knowledge provided in schools which does not meet the needs for those competing in the entrance exam system." This is a commentary quote, not a TDRI quantitative study. — [Chiang Mai CityLife](https://www.chiangmaicitylife.com/citylife-articles/thailands-shadow-education-2/)
- A qualitative study of grade 10–12 Thai students finds the decision to take shadow education is strongly shaped by social origin and economic, social and cultural status. The effect is strongest for better-resourced families, and the authors frame tutoring as a perceived driver of educational inequality. — [Journal of International Studies (TCI), "The Decision for Shadow Education Participation and the Perceived Formation of Educational Inequality in Thailand"](https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jis/article/view/247257)
- ADB's regional report on shadow education covers Thailand among Asian systems and frames tutoring as a policy issue for equity. — [ADB, Shadow Education (Bray & Lykins)](https://www.adb.org/publications/shadow-education-private-supplementary-tutoring-and-its-implications-policy-makers-asia) **[Thailand-specific figures not extracted]**
- English private tutoring in Thailand is studied as a distinct sector. — [rEFLections journal, "Local Perspectives on English Language Teaching in Private Tutoring in Thailand"](https://so05.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/reflections/article/view/256348) **[snippet only]**
- SAT tutoring for inter programmes exists as a niche. Campus Genius Center runs intensive SAT courses aimed at international-programme entry, though the article gives no fees. — [MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/qol/detail/9630000124765) **[TH, snippet only]**. Parents on Thai forums recommend online 1:1 SAT tutoring (e.g. Gimme Eng), with no prices given. — [cosmenet.in.th thread](https://www.cosmenet.in.th/community/36/63569) **[TH, forum, low reliability]**
- Enconcept has historically used free TCAS lessons as promotions (2019, valued at 1,000 baht). — [TruePlookPanya](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/72105-blo-) **[TH, dated]**
- Online marketplace benchmark: one Preply SAT tutor lists USD 25 per 50-minute lesson. — [Preply](https://preply.com/th/tutor/8165776) **[single listing, not representative]**

### Inferences

- Even without exact prices, SAT prep, IELTS prep and inter-interview coaching are paid niches clustered in Bangkok and in online 1:1 formats. Applicants without money or a Bangkok base face a preparation gap that the free exam reform does not close.
- For framing, BIR Tutor should describe itself as a free complement to school and self-study rather than a substitute for or competitor with commercial schools. It should avoid claims about its effect on admission outcomes.

### Gaps

- No verified 2026 baht prices for OnDemand, Enconcept, SAT, IELTS or interview-prep courses. Recommend a manual check of provider websites and LINE OAs, recorded with date.
- No World Bank or TDRI quantitative report on Thai tutoring spending by income quintile or region was located. Thai NSO Socio-Economic Survey household education spending is the likely source, not checked.
- SAT registration fee (College Board, plus any non-US fee) and IELTS fee in Thailand were not verified this session.

## 3. Equity: Bangkok vs provinces, income gaps, PISA 2022

### Takeaway

PISA 2022 shows Thailand at its lowest-ever averages, with large rural–urban gaps (Thai press reports rural students 53–70 points behind, about three school years). Learning gaps by wealth and language are documented by UNICEF/UNESCO. This supports targeting provincial and lower-income applicants explicitly.

### Cited Findings

- Thailand's PISA 2022 averages fell in all three subjects and were lower than in any previous assessment. Students scored below the OECD average in maths, reading and science. Over 2012–2022 the decline was about 30 points in maths and science and about 60 in reading. — [OECD PISA 2022 Country Note: Thailand](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-i-and-ii-country-notes_ed6fbcc5-en/thailand_6138f4af-en.html) **[snippet only]**
- The gap between top and bottom 10% performers narrowed in maths, and did not change significantly in reading and science. — [OECD Country Note](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-i-and-ii-country-notes_ed6fbcc5-en/thailand_6138f4af-en.html) **[snippet only]**
- Students in rural schools (communities under 3,000 people) scored on average 53–70 points lower than urban and suburban peers across maths, science and reading, described as about three school years behind. — [Thairath English, scoop](https://en.thairath.co.th/scoop/theissue/2908035); [Thairath infographic](https://en.thairath.co.th/scoop/infographic/2907838) **[news, not OECD primary; verify against OECD Vol. II]**
- UNICEF says learning gaps persist between urban and rural, rich and poor, and Thai-speaking and non-Thai-speaking children. — [UNICEF Thailand, "Fix Thailand's education before it's too late"](https://www.unicef.org/thailand/stories/fix-thailands-education-its-too-late)
- Thailand's Equitable Education Fund (EEF Thailand, กสศ.) has published analysis of PISA 2022 findings. — [EEF Thailand research](https://research.eef.or.th/the-pisa-2022-findings-assess/) **[not read in full]**
- Thai research on COVID-19 effects on educational inequality, using PISA maths. — [NER journal (TCI)](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/NER/article/view/287480) **[not read]**

### Inferences

- PISA measures 15-year-olds generally, not inter-programme applicants, who are a selected and generally advantaged group. It supports _outreach_ targeting (provincial schools, non-Bangkok applicants, students without tutoring access). It does not describe the typical BIR applicant.
- The English essay and English interview compound the gap. English-medium preparation is concentrated in Bangkok and in private and international schools (inference, not quantified here).

### Gaps

- ESCS socio-economic gradient for Thailand in PISA 2022 not extracted.
- No data found on the regional or school-type composition of BIR or TU inter-programme intakes.

## 4. Internet and device access, mobile-first usage, LINE

### Takeaway

Thailand is overwhelmingly mobile-first. About 98% of internet users browse on phones, and LINE reaches about 56 million users (about 85% of internet users). A web service should be phone-first, low-bandwidth, and reachable from LINE.

### Cited Findings

- LINE has about 56 million monthly active users in Thailand (Digital 2025), equal to about 85.4% of internet users. LINE Thailand's own 2026 business materials also cite 56 million users and 12 billion Official Account conversations. — [Meltwater, social media statistics Thailand](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/social-media-statistics-thailand); [Relevant Audience, LINE Thailand 2026](https://www.relevantaudience.com/digital-marketing-en/line-thailand-business-solutions-2026/)
- About 98% of Thai internet users browse via mobile phones (2025). Mobile accounts for 63.3% of internet time. There are 99.5 million mobile connections (139% of population, counting SIMs not people). — [Meltwater](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/social-media-statistics-thailand) **[industry, not official]**
- Older data: mobile access is considerably higher among younger groups, and nearly three-quarters of Thai mobile subscribers were under 24. — [We Are Social](https://wearesocial.com/uk/?p=5808) **[dated]**

### Inferences

- Design for a mid-range Android on mobile data. Small page weight, no heavy video autoplay, and resilience to dropped connections (save drafts locally) matter for essay practice.
- LINE is the expected contact channel. A LINE OA for reminders and links is culturally standard. Collecting LINE IDs, though, is personal data (see PDPA section).
- Voice-recorded mock interviews depend on microphone permissions and upload size on mobile data. Offer a text alternative.

### Gaps

- No official NSO ICT household survey figure for upper-secondary students (ages 15–18) by region or income was obtained. Laptop access (important for long essays) is unknown.

## 5. Evidence on peer, near-peer and volunteer tutoring and what makes it work

### Takeaway

Tutoring is among the best-evidenced interventions (pooled about 0.29–0.37 SD). Effects are larger for trained or paid tutors, in-school scheduling, and higher dosage. Volunteer online tutoring by university students works when it is structured, frequent (about 3 h/week) and 1:1. Low-dosage volunteer pilots show small, non-significant effects. Peer tutoring is cheap and moderately effective (EEF +5 to +6 months) when tutors are trained and sessions run in intensive blocks.

### Cited Findings

- Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan meta-analysis of experimental PreK–12 tutoring: pooled effect of 0.37 SD in the 2020 NBER/EdWorkingPaper. — [NBER w27476](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476); [EdWorkingPapers ai20-267](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai20-267)
- A later version (2024, "The Promise of Tutoring...") reports a pooled 0.288 SD (SE 0.029). The difference from 0.37 is not explained in the snippets. Cite the published version. — [Stanford NSSA study page](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/promise-tutoring-prek-12-learning-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis-experimental-evidence)
- Moderators: effects are stronger for teacher and paraprofessional tutors than for non-professional or parent tutors, and stronger for programmes held during school than after school. — [NBER w27476](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476)
- Kraft, List, Livingston & Sadoff (2022), online tutoring by college volunteers, an RCT of 560 US 6th–8th graders. Effects about 0.07 SD maths and 0.04 SD reading, not statistically significant (ITT 0.053, TOT 0.066 per NSSA). Evidence is consistent with a dosage model where more hours give larger effects. Very low cost. Note that Falken is not an author. — [AEA Papers & Proceedings 112:614–18](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/pandp.20221038); [NSSA summary](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/online-tutoring-college-volunteers-experimental-evidence-pilot-program)
- Carlana & La Ferrara, Tutoring Online Program (TOP), Italy. Volunteer university students tutored disadvantaged middle-schoolers fully online. Three hours per week of 1:1 tutoring raised maths by 0.22 SD (2020) and 0.20 SD (2022). Higher dosage gave stronger effects, while group tutoring gave smaller effects. It improved aspirations, socio-emotional skills and well-being, but only during school closures. Tutors gained empathy. — [AEA RCT Registry 5607](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/5607); [Bocconi IRIS](https://iris.unibocconi.it/handle/11565/4081676); [openICPSR data "Apart but Connected"](https://www.openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/228584/version/V1/view). An IDB/HKS summary cites 0.26 SD, with the outcome definition unclear. — [HKS](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/italy-tutoring-online-program-top-successful-global-experience)
- EEF (England) Teaching and Learning Toolkit, peer tutoring: +5 months in a 2026 secondary summary, and +6 months in the 2013 version. Very low cost (under £80 per learner per year; EEF-funded programmes about £10 per pupil, with lower impact). Tutor training is a key ingredient, and intensive 4–10 week blocks appear to maximise impact for tutors and tutees. — [Structural Learning guide to EEF Toolkit](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/eef-teaching-learning-toolkit-guide); [Education Scotland mirror of EEF peer tutoring page](https://nrs.preservation.mirrorweb.com/public/20191211024427mp_/https://education.gov.scot/improvement/eef-toolkit/peer-tutoring/) **[secondary; verify on educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk]**
- Stanford NSSA tutoring toolkit gives design guidance on tutor recruitment, training and scheduling. — [NSSA toolkit section 3](https://nssa.stanford.edu/toolkit-tutoring-programs/section-3) **[not read in full]**

### Inferences (design implications)

- Dosage and structure matter more than tutor expertise. A short intensive block before each BIR milestone (about 4–8 weeks before the 15 Nov 2026 interview, and again before the 21 Mar 2027 written exam) fits the "intensive block" evidence.
- Train volunteer tutors with a fixed session script, rubric-based essay feedback, and question banks written by the association, not improvised. This substitutes for the professional-tutor advantage.
- Prefer 1:1 or very small groups for interview practice. Larger-group webinars suit general knowledge content but should be expected to have smaller effects.
- Near-peer tutors (current BIR students) gain too (empathy, per TOP), which is a legitimate benefit to report to the faculty.
- Kraft et al. show cheap low-dosage volunteer programmes can be near-null. Do not promise outcomes, and track attendance and dosage.

### Gaps

- No meta-analysis specific to admissions-test or interview coaching by peers was found. Most evidence is K–8 maths and reading, so transfer to 17–18-year-olds preparing essays and interviews is an inference.
- Cross-age tutoring meta-analyses (e.g. Cohen, Kulik & Kulik 1982; Leung 2015) were not retrieved this session.

## 6. Fairness and conflict of interest (student association tutoring its own programme)

### Takeaway

No Thammasat or TCAS rule specific to student-run prep for one's own programme was found in this pass. Generic risks are clear, though. The service must not imply insider knowledge, access to exam content, or influence over selection, and student tutors should be separated from any admissions role.

### Cited Findings

- Thai universities and tutoring brands routinely run free TCAS prep events and open-house style sessions. Enconcept, for example, ran free TCAS lessons and in-person TCAS prep events as promotions. — [TruePlookPanya](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/72105-blo-); [TruePlookPanya](https://www.trueplookpanya.com/knowledge/content/74458-blo-) **[TH, dated 2019]**
- TU has published a Cambridge case study on using BMAT for admissions, showing TU's use of external standardised tests as one fairness mechanism. — [Cambridge English, BMAT Thammasat case study](https://www.cambridgeEnglish.org/Images/618714-bmat-thammasat-university-case-study.pdf) **[not read; relevance limited to medicine]**

### Inferences (design implications)

- Put a prominent disclaimer in both languages. It should say the service is run by students, is independent of the admissions committee, has no access to exam questions, and that using it neither helps nor harms an application.
- Tutors should not include anyone who sits on interview panels, invigilates, or handles applications. Get written faculty acknowledgement of the service.
- Practice questions should be original or from public sources. Don't use "reconstructed" past papers recalled by students who sat the exam, which may breach exam confidentiality.
- Free and open access (no selection of who gets help) avoids creating a privileged in-group.
- Student-run "ค่ายติว" camps and "TU open house" sessions are common precedents, but no source was retrieved here (see Gaps).

### Gaps

- No TU regulation, TCAS (ทปอ.) rule or Faculty of Political Science policy on student-run prep for admissions was found. Recommend asking the faculty's undergraduate office directly.
- No sourced precedent of a Thai student-association tutoring camp (ค่ายติว) with published conflict-of-interest policies was retrieved.

## 7. PDPA (B.E. 2562) constraints: minors, sensitive content, AI APIs, cross-border transfer, retention

### Takeaway

Most applicants are 17–19 and therefore minors (majority at 20). PDPC guidelines treat ages 10–20 as a group where parental consent is not _always_ required. Under-10s always need it. Whether a 17–19-year-old can consent alone depends on Civil and Commercial Code capacity rules, so a cautious design adds a guardian acknowledgement or uses a non-consent lawful basis. Sending essays or voice to a foreign AI API is a cross-border transfer (s.28/29). It needs an adequate destination, SCCs/BCRs under the PDPC notifications in force from 24 March 2024, or explicit informed consent. Notices must be clear, state the legal basis and disclose cross-border transfers.

### Cited Findings

- PDPC consent guidelines (issued 7 Sep 2022) define two age groups. For under 10, parental consent is compulsory. For 10 to under 20, parental consent is generally not required in all circumstances. Controllers should use appropriate identification and age-verification measures. — [Tilleke & Gibbins, PDPC consent and notification guidelines](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-issues-guidelines-on-pdpa-consent-and-notification-requirements/2/)
- PwC summary: parental consent is needed for 10–19-year-olds where the minor is not sui juris, meaning the act is not one a minor can perform alone. — [PwC Legal, Thailand data protection overview](https://legal.pwc.de/content/im-fokus/thailand-global-data-protection-law-overview.pdf) **[snippet only]**
- Consent must be obtained before or at collection, after informing the data subject of purposes and details, by a clear affirmative act. Notices must be clear and easy to understand, state the legal basis, and give details of any cross-border transfers. A prominent hyperlink to the policy is acceptable. — [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-issues-guidelines-on-pdpa-consent-and-notification-requirements/2/)
- Section 28: transfers abroad require the destination to have adequate protection, with exceptions including the data subject's consent (after being informed of inadequate standards), contract performance, legal compliance and vital interests. Section 29 covers BCRs and appropriate safeguards (SCCs). PDPC notifications under ss.28–29 came into force 24 March 2024. Administrative fines run up to THB 5 million. — [DataGuidance](https://www.DataGuidance.com/opinion/thailand-operationalising-pdpa-data-transfers-and); [Mondaq, Thailand's new legislation on cross-border transfer](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/data-protection/1410022/thailands-new-legislation-on-cross-border-transfer-of-personal-data) **[secondary summaries]**
- Tilleke's site lists an article (14 Oct 2026) on PDPC AI guidelines, described as advisory rather than legally binding. — [Tilleke insights listing](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-issues-guidelines-on-pdpa-consent-and-notification-requirements/2/) **[article itself not read]**
- One search summary mentioned a PDPA amendment draft in public consultation (16 Jul to 15 Aug 2026). On fetching the Tilleke page this **could not be confirmed**, since the 2026 consultations listed there concerned electricity licensing and Vietnamese crypto reporting. Treat it as unverified.

### Inferences (design implications)

- Minimise data. Use a display name and email or LINE login only. Don't collect national ID, school name or grades unless needed. Make accounts optional for reading materials.
- For 17–19-year-olds, use a layered bilingual notice plus the applicant's own consent, with an optional guardian acknowledgement checkbox for under-20s. Avoid relying solely on minor consent for anything beyond the core service.
- Essays and voice recordings: never send to third-party AI APIs without explicit, separate, opt-in consent that names the provider and country. Prefer providers with SCCs or DPAs and no-training/zero-retention terms. Voice is not "sensitive data" under s.26 unless used for biometric identification, but treat it conservatively.
- Retention: delete essays and recordings after the cycle ends (e.g. after Round 4 closes, June 2027) or on request. State this in the notice. Provide easy deletion and export.
- Appoint a named contact for data requests. A full DPO may not be legally required for a small association, but this was not verified.
- Check the 14 Oct 2026 PDPC AI guidance before launch.

### Gaps

- Official English translation of PDPA s.20 (minors), s.26 (sensitive data) and s.37 (security/breach notification) not fetched from pdpc.or.th. Statute wording should be verified.
- Whether a student association (not a juristic person) is the "data controller", or whether Thammasat University is, remains unclear. Needs advice from TU's data protection office.
- Unconfirmed whether a 2026 PDPA amendment is pending.

## 8. Accessibility and bilingual (Thai/English) plain-language design

### Takeaway

Little Thai-specific evidence was gathered in this pass. PDPC guidance requires notices in clear, easy-to-understand language, and the population is mobile-first and largely Thai-dominant. A Thai-first bilingual design with plain language and WCAG 2.2 AA conformance is the defensible default.

### Cited Findings

- PDPA notices must use clear, easy-to-understand language. — [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-issues-guidelines-on-pdpa-consent-and-notification-requirements/2/)
- Learning gaps exist between Thai-speaking and non-Thai-speaking children (e.g. ethnic-minority and border-area students). — [UNICEF Thailand](https://www.unicef.org/thailand/stories/fix-thailands-education-its-too-late)
- Mobile-first usage (98% browse on phones). — [Meltwater](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/social-media-statistics-thailand)

### Inferences

- Provide Thai and English for all instructions, consent text and feedback. Keep practice materials in English where the exam is in English (essay, interview) but explain the tasks in Thai.
- Thai text has no word spaces, so line-breaking (e.g. `word-break: keep-all` is inappropriate; use proper Thai segmentation or `lang="th"` so browsers apply dictionary-based breaking) and screen-reader support need testing. This matches the repo's own CLAUDE.md warning about Thai rewrapping.
- Captions or transcripts for any video, text alternatives to audio practice, adequate font size for Thai diacritics, and offline-tolerant forms.

### Gaps

- No Thai government plain-language standard or Thai web accessibility regulation was retrieved. Thailand has accessibility guidance from ETDA/DGA (e.g. Thai WCAG-based standard มอ.), not verified this session.
- No Thai-specific research on bilingual education UX was found.
