# Evidence-based preparation for three BIR admission components (EFL argumentative essay, Digital SAT EBRW, English interview) plus the politics/IR MCQ

Scope note: research run on 19 Oct 2026 with a budget of about 15 tool calls. Several full texts (Writing Next PDF, Kang & Han 2015, the Maurer coaching papers) could not be fetched. Figures marked "recalled, unverified" are in the Gaps sections, not in Cited Findings, so the report writer can decide whether to verify them or leave them out.

## 1. Writing instruction meta-analyses (Writing Next, SRSD, models, sentence combining, planning, timed practice)

### Takeaway

Explicit strategy instruction for planning and revising, especially SRSD, is the most consistently supported writing intervention for grades 4 to 12. Summarisation, collaborative writing, specific product goals and sentence combining come next. Studying models helps but has one of the smaller effects. No SRSD meta-analysis specific to adult or EFL learners was found, so applying SRSD to Thai EFL applicants is an extrapolation.

### Cited Findings

- Writing Next (Graham & Perin 2007; 142 studies, recommendations aimed at grades 4 to 12) ranks 11 elements from largest to smallest effect on writing quality. In order they are writing strategies (explicit planning, revising and editing), summarisation, collaborative writing, specific product goals, word processing, sentence combining, prewriting, inquiry activities, process writing approach, study of models, and writing for content learning. — [AdLit summary of Writing Next](https://www.adlit.org/topics/writing/summary-writing-next)
- A secondary summary gives Writing Next effect sizes of about 0.82 for strategy instruction (20 studies), 0.75 for peer assistance and 0.70 for product goals. This is a blog source, so treat it as secondhand. — [hickstro.org notes on Writing Next](https://hickstro.org/2007/06/)
- Conventional effect-size benchmarks are 0.3 small, 0.5 moderate and 0.8 large. — [hickstro.org](https://hickstro.org/2007/06/)
- One meta-analysis reports an average effect size of 1.33 for SRSD versus 0.76 for strategy instruction without SRSD, among children and adolescents with learning disabilities. — [Texas Center for Learning Disabilities](https://texasldcenter.org/?p=338)
- A synthesis of 23 single-subject studies of writing interventions for students with learning disabilities found high effect sizes for both SRSD and non-SRSD strategy instruction. — [University of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/COMMON-CHARACTERISTICS-OF-WRITING-INTERVENTIONS-FOR/9984371080402771)
- A grade 1 to 6 meta-analysis (Graham, McKeown, Kiuhara & Harris 2012) gives an effect size of 1.02 for strategy instruction, 0.50 for adding self-regulation to strategy instruction and 0.59 for text-structure instruction. — [ASU record of the elementary meta-analysis](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-writing-instruction-for-students-in-the-elemen/)
- One table gives effect sizes of 0.80 for adult feedback and 0.37 for peer or self feedback on writing. The original source is not clear in the snippet. — [Texas LD Center](https://texasldcenter.org/?p=311)
- SRSD-affiliated pages cite +1.22 for at-risk students. They also say SRSD effects hold for students with and without learning disabilities, across ages, teachers and genres. These are promotional pages, not the primary studies. — [srsdonline.org](https://srsdonline.org/?p=1559); [srsdonline.org, science of writing](https://srsdonline.org/science-of-writing/)
- A 2023 Graham et al. meta-analysis of writing treatments for grades 6 to 12 is described as finding that SRSD significantly improves writing across those grades. Effect sizes were not retrieved. — [srsdonline.org](https://srsdonline.org/evidence-based-writing-solution-hiding-in-plain-sight-srsd/)

### Inferences

- For a 60% essay component, the strongest evidence-based core is explicit, taught strategies for planning (for example a POW+TREE-style argument mnemonic, or a Toulmin plan) and revising. They should be modelled by the tutor, memorised, practised with gradual release, and paired with self-regulation (goal-setting, self-monitoring and self-instruction). That is the SRSD sequence.
- Summarisation practice of IR readings is a double win. It ranks high for writing quality and it builds the domain knowledge the 40% MCQ needs.
- Specific product goals line up with rubric-referenced targets, for example "include one rebuttal paragraph" or "use two specific examples".
- Sentence combining is a cheap, evidence-backed fix for the simple-sentence and run-on profile common in Thai EFL writing (see section 3).
- Models and exemplars are worth using, but analysing a model essay is lower-yield than teaching the strategy that produced it.
- Timed writing practice: I found no meta-analysis isolating timed practice as an intervention. A sensible inference is that timed rehearsal mainly builds pacing and planning-under-pressure for a 2-hour paper (roughly 70 minutes for the essay if time is split by weight). Strategy instruction should come first, with timing layered on top.

### Gaps

- Exact Writing Next effect sizes were not verified because the PDF returned 403. Recalled, unverified: strategy instruction d≈0.82, summarisation ≈0.82, collaborative writing ≈0.75, product goals ≈0.70, word processing ≈0.55, sentence combining ≈0.50, prewriting ≈0.32, inquiry ≈0.32, process approach ≈0.32, study of models ≈0.25, writing for content learning ≈0.23, with grammar instruction negative (≈−0.32).
- Graham, Harris & colleagues' 2016 and 2023 grade 6 to 12 effect sizes were not retrieved.
- No meta-analysis of SRSD with adult, university-entry or EFL writers was found. Single EFL SRSD studies probably exist (Iran, Turkey, China) but were not searched.
- No evidence was found on timed-essay practice as a standalone intervention.

## 2. Argument structure (Toulmin, claim–evidence–reasoning, counterargument), rubrics, self- and peer assessment

### Takeaway

Rubric use has a moderate positive effect on academic performance (g≈0.45). Self-assessment reliably improves self-regulated learning and self-efficacy. The evidence that rubric-referenced self- and peer assessment improves writing quality specifically was not isolated in what I could retrieve.

### Cited Findings

- Panadero, Jonsson, Pinedo & Fernández-Castilla (2023), a rubric meta-analysis, found a moderate positive effect of rubrics on academic performance (g = 0.45, k = 21, m = 54), with smaller pooled effects on self-regulated learning and self-efficacy. Whether self or peer assessment was used was tested as a moderator. — [Deusto repository: Effects of rubrics on academic performance, self-regulated learning, and self-efficacy](https://deustoteka.deusto.es/items/59022074-df96-4c6a-8c52-3aa513c0ae3e)
- Panadero, Jonsson & Botella (2017), _Educational Research Review_ 22, 74–98, pooled 19 studies (N = 2,305). Self-assessment effects on self-regulated learning were 0.23, 0.65 and 0.43 across three meta-analyses, and the effect on self-efficacy was 0.73. — [Deusto portal record](https://portalinvestigacion.deusto.es/documentos/6326545dd50fae52cd317f7b)
- Yan, Lao, Panadero & Fernández-Castilla (2026) pooled 353 effect sizes from 75 studies and found positive effects of self-assessment on learning strategies and affective outcomes (Hedges' g 0.205 to 0.683). A 2022 Yan et al. meta-analysis covers self- and peer-assessment effects on academic performance, but its effect sizes were not retrieved. — [Deusto repository](https://deustoteka.deusto.es/items/0a1173b9-ed0a-40b9-a330-f02d56a2cec8); [record of the 2022 self/peer-assessment meta-analysis](https://research.science.eus/documentos/635da267f50cf01a7961105a)
- Writing Next ranks collaborative writing (peers working together to plan, draft, revise and edit) third of 11 elements. — [AdLit](https://www.adlit.org/topics/writing/summary-writing-next)

### Inferences

- Use one analytic rubric throughout preparation. IELTS Task 2's four criteria (Task Response; Coherence and Cohesion; Lexical Resource; Grammatical Range and Accuracy) are a familiar, public, well-described option for Thai students. Add an IR content criterion covering accurate concepts and specific real-world examples, because the BIR essay is content-bearing.
- Teach Toulmin (claim, grounds, warrant, plus qualifier and rebuttal) or claim–evidence–reasoning as the paragraph engine, with an explicit counterargument-and-rebuttal paragraph. This maps onto both the "strategy" and "product goal" elements above.
- Self-assessment against the rubric before tutor feedback is likely to raise self-efficacy and calibration. Peer marking of anonymised scripts can follow once students know the rubric.

### Gaps

- I did not retrieve the official IELTS, AP or IB descriptors themselves. They are public on ielts.org and the College Board AP site, so the report writer can link them directly.
- No retrieved meta-analysis was specific to argumentative-writing structure instruction. Toulmin-based instruction studies in EFL exist but were not searched.
- Double, McGrane & Hopfenbeck (2020) on peer assessment (recalled g≈0.31, unverified) was not retrieved.

## 3. Thai learners' English writing difficulties and written corrective feedback (WCF)

### Takeaway

Thai EFL studies consistently report errors in singular and plural nouns, articles (especially omitting "the"), subject–verb agreement, punctuation, fragments and run-ons. These are mostly attributed to L1 transfer, since Thai has no articles, inflection or plural marking, but intralingual errors also occur. Meta-analytic evidence shows WCF improves L2 written accuracy with a small-to-moderate effect, and focused feedback tends to beat unfocused feedback.

### Cited Findings

- In Thai EFL writing, wrong singular and plural noun use, omission of "the" and subject–verb disagreement were the most frequent grammatical errors. — [rEFLections (Thai journal) article](https://so05.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/reflections/article/download/248862/169222/872512)
- Among Thai English majors, the most frequent errors were punctuation, articles, subject–verb agreement, spelling, capitalisation and fragments. — [IDEAS/ELT journal, 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ibn/eltjnl/v10y2017i3p101.html)
- A study of argumentative essays by Thai undergraduates (surface strategy taxonomy) found misinformation errors were most common, followed by omission, addition and misordering. It linked some errors to a "double subject" (topic-comment) construction carried over from Thai. — [Journal of Language and Linguistics (JLA), TCI](https://so07.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JLA/article/view/10848)
- Comparing four essay genres, subject–verb agreement errors were most frequent in point-counterpoint (argumentative-type) essays. — [MPJH, TCI](https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/mpjh/article/view/268155)
- In paragraphs by Thai accounting students, basic subject–verb agreement accounted for 72% of agreement errors. — [Rangsit Journal of Educational Studies](https://rsujournals.rsu.ac.th/index.php/RJES/article/view/2604)
- Error causes are attributed to a mix of interlingual (L1 Thai) and intralingual interference. — [PSU thesis chapter](https://kb.psu.ac.th/psukb/bitstream/2553/1495/5/271440_ch4.pdf)
- Kang & Han (2015), _Modern Language Journal_ 99(1), 1–18, pooled 21 experimental or quasi-experimental studies (1980 to 2013). WCF improved L2 grammatical accuracy with a "small to moderate" overall effect. — cited via [Journal of Language and Education, HSE (2022)](https://jle.hse.ru/article/view/16516)
- A research synthesis of 34 unfocused-feedback studies notes that focused feedback has been found more effective than unfocused feedback (Kao & Wible 2014). — [Journal of Language and Education, HSE](https://jle.hse.ru/article/view/16516)
- rEFLections (a Thai journal) has published a review of WCF research in L2 writing. — [rEFLections review](https://so05.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/reflections/article/view/275269)

### Inferences

- An error-pattern checklist for Thai writers might read: articles (a/the/zero), plural -s, subject–verb agreement, tense consistency (Thai marks time lexically), run-ons and comma splices, fragments, and topic-comment or double-subject sentences ("Thailand, its economy is…"). It can double as a proofreading routine in the last 5 minutes of the timed essay.
- Use focused WCF on 2 or 3 recurring error types per student per cycle, rather than marking everything. Pair it with a personal error log and resubmission.
- Rhetorical organisation matters too. Thai writers are often described as delaying the thesis or writing in an indirect, circular style (contrastive rhetoric). Explicit "thesis in the first paragraph, topic sentence first" templates address this. I did not retrieve a Thai-specific contrastive-rhetoric study this session, so treat this as an inference.

### Gaps

- Kang & Han's exact effect sizes (recalled g≈0.68 overall, with larger effects for intermediate learners and for direct feedback, unverified) and their focused/unfocused breakdown were not retrieved. The DOI is 10.1111/modl.12189.
- Bitchener and Ferris primary studies were not retrieved.
- PASAA and LEARN journal studies were not specifically retrieved. No Thai contrastive-rhetoric study was retrieved.

## 4. SAT EBRW (Digital SAT): coaching effects, format, and the 2026 test-date problem

### Takeaway

Controlled studies put commercial coaching effects at roughly 6 to 20 points on the verbal section, far smaller than marketing claims. Uncontrolled studies overstate effects 4 to 5 times. The Khan Academy "20 hours = 115 points" figure is correlational. Critically, as of 19 Oct 2026 the only SAT date that reports before Round 1 closes on 6 Nov 2026 is 3 Oct 2026, which has already happened. The 7 Nov date falls after the deadline.

### Cited Findings

- Format: Reading & Writing has 54 questions in 64 minutes, split into two adaptive 32-minute modules of 27 scored plus 2 pretest questions each. Module 2 difficulty depends on Module 1. There are four content domains: Craft and Structure; Information and Ideas; Standard English Conventions; Expression of Ideas. Passages are short (about 25 to 150 words) with one question each. — [College Board R&W guide](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/psat-nmsqt-student-guide/prepare/reading-writing); [Tutela Prep](https://www.tutelaprep.com/blog/all-about-sat-digital-suite-reading--writing/)
- Craft and Structure accounts for 13 to 15 questions and covers words in context, text structure and purpose, and cross-text connections. — [College Board R&W guide](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/psat-nmsqt-student-guide/prepare/reading-writing)
- SAT Weekend dates for 2026–27 (they apply to US and international students alike):

  | Test date  | Registration deadline | Late registration and changes |
  | ---------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------- |
  | 3 Oct 2026 | 18 Sep                | 22 Sep                        |
  | 7 Nov 2026 | 23 Oct                | 27 Oct                        |
  | 5 Dec 2026 | 20 Nov                | 24 Nov                        |
  | 6 Mar 2027 |                       |                               |
  | 1 May 2027 |                       |                               |
  | 5 Jun 2027 |                       |                               |

  The page notes that most 3 Oct scores are already out. — [College Board dates and deadlines](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/dates-deadlines)

- Conflict: Test Ninjas lists 22 Aug and 12 Sep 2026 dates, while the fetched College Board page reported none for Aug or Sep 2026. The fetch may only have shown upcoming dates. — [Test Ninjas](https://www.test-ninjas.com/sat-test-dates); [College Board](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/dates-deadlines)
- Domingue & Briggs (2009) used ELS:2002 data with regression and propensity-score matching. Coaching effects were about 11 to 15 points on math and 6 to 9 on verbal, and only the math effects were significant. Effects were larger for students with challenging coursework and higher socioeconomic status. — [Stanford CEPA abstract](https://cepa.stanford.edu/content/using-linear-regression-and-propensity-score-matching-estimate-effect-coaching-sat); [full text](https://ojs.lib.ua.edu/glmj/article/view/250)
- Briggs, using NELS:88 data, found commercial coaching worth about 3 to 20 points on verbal and 10 to 28 on math. Effects in uncontrolled studies were 4 to 5 times larger than in controlled or randomised ones. Meta-analytic models of coaching studies predict new studies with large error. — [Briggs, NEPC](https://www.nepc.colorado.edu/sites/default/files/Briggs_SATCoachingBiasandCasualInference.pdf); [Briggs meta-analysis case study](https://www.colorado.edu/education/sites/default/files/attached-files/Briggs_Meta-Analysis.pdf)
- College Board has stated coaching effects of 15 to 30 points on math and 10 to 20 on verbal, depending on programme length. — [Briggs comment, NEPC](https://www.nepc.colorado.edu/sites/default/files/Briggs_CommentJackKaplansNewStudy.pdf)
- College Board and Khan Academy (2017) looked at about 250,000 students, comparing PSAT/NMSQT and SAT scores. Twenty hours on Official SAT Practice was associated with an average 115-point total gain, about double that of non-users. Zero hours was associated with about 60 points and 6 hours with about 90. The release frames this as an association, not a causal effect, and it predates the digital SAT. — [College Board newsroom](https://newsroom.collegeboard.org/new-data-links-20-hours-personalized-official-sat-practice-khan-academy-115-point-average-score); [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/studying-for-the-sat-for-20-hours-on-khan-academy/)

### Inferences

- Timing for the 15 Nov 2026 interview cohort: an applicant without an existing score cannot realistically produce one for Round 1. The 7 Nov sitting is after the 6 Nov close, and scores take further time. The tutor plan should treat Round 1 as for students who already hold scores (or who sat on 3 Oct or earlier). For later applicants it should target the 2027 dates, for example 6 Mar, 1 May or 5 Jun 2027, ahead of the next cycle. Check whether BIR accepts "scores pending" or scores released after the application closes. That is not verified.
- Since the 3 Oct sitting's scores are already out, students in this cycle should now focus on the interview and on Round 3 instead of further SAT prep.
- What plausibly moves EBRW scores: official adaptive practice tests in Bluebook, plus targeted practice on the four domains. Standard English Conventions (boundaries/punctuation, form/structure/sense) is rule-based and quickly learnable, and it overlaps with Thai learners' agreement and punctuation errors. Vocabulary-in-context and evidence questions need broader reading. Expect modest gains (tens of points) from short preparation, with larger gains only from sustained reading and practice.

### Gaps

- Powers & Rock (1999) figures were not retrieved (recalled about +8 verbal / +18 math, unverified).
- No RCT of Digital SAT (2024 onwards) preparation was found. Bluebook-specific efficacy data was not found.
- Score release dates for Oct to Dec 2026 were not listed on the fetched page.
- Whether BIR Round 1 accepts the 7 Nov score or "pending" status was not checked; the BIR announcement would settle it.

## 5. Interview preparation (mock interviews, coaching, anxiety, L2 fluency)

### Takeaway

Evidence on interview coaching for selection is thin and mixed. Medical-school MMI studies show little or no score benefit from commercial coaching, though repeat exposure to similar stations can help. L2 anxiety correlates moderately and negatively with L2 achievement (r≈−.36), which supports anxiety-reducing rehearsal (repeated mock interviews, recording) as a reasonable, low-risk approach.

### Cited Findings

- Griffin et al. (2008, _MJA_) studied 287 University of Western Sydney medicine applicants on UMAT and MMI coaching and retesting. — [MJA](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2008/189/5/does-practice-make-perfect-effect-coaching-and-retesting-selection-tests-used)
- MJA (2015): there was no statistical difference between coached (n = 122) and uncoached (n = 222) interviewees on the three UMAT scores or MMI scores, and coached applicants were no more likely to gain entry. — [MJA 2015](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2015/203/9/reducing-impact-coaching-selection-medicine)
- Short-term prior access to MMI questions did not affect performance at a Canadian school. In Australia, re-sitting the MMI a year later improved scores on stations the same as or similar to the originals. — [MJA 2014](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2014/201/7/multiple-mini-interview-performance-repeat-applicants-medical-school-admission)
- Maurer, Solamon & Troxtel (1998), "Relationship of coaching with performance in situational employment interviews," _Journal of Applied Psychology_, DOI 10.1037/0021-9010.83.1.128. The bibliographic record was confirmed; the findings were not retrieved. — [PubPeer record](https://pubpeer.com/publications/8EA748238ECC8D687FF04AB59E768B)
- Teimouri, Goetze & Plonsky (2019) pooled 97 reports (105 samples, N = 19,933, 23 countries). The mean correlation between L2 anxiety and achievement was r = −.36. — [NAU record](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/second-language-anxiety-and-achievement/); [Frontiers in Psychology 2022, citing it](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.1031514/full)

### Inferences

- Use structured mock interviews with a panel format, a time limit and unseen prompts drawn from the likely question families: motivation for BIR or IR, a current-affairs issue, a Thai foreign-policy question, and follow-up "why?" probing. Video-record them and self-review against a short rubric covering content, structure (PREP or claim-reason-example), fluency and interaction.
- Repeated exposure reduces novelty-driven anxiety, and the MMI repeat-exposure finding suggests familiarity with the format helps. Memorised scripts are unlikely to transfer, given the null coaching effects.
- Fluency work with 4/3/2-style repeated timed retelling and prepared "chunks" for opening and hedging is a reasonable way to build L2 speaking automaticity. This is inferred from SLA practice and was not verified this session.

### Gaps

- Maurer et al. (1998, 2001) and Tross & Maurer (2008) findings were not retrieved. Recalled, unverified: coaching attendance associated with better structured-interview performance, partly via organisation of answers.
- No speaking-specific anxiety effect size was retrieved. The FLCAS (Horwitz, Horwitz & Cope 1986) itself was not fetched.
- No studies were found on English-medium university admission interviews for Thai or Asian applicants.

## 6. Political-knowledge MCQ (40% of TCAS 3 exam)

### Takeaway

I found no public specification of the BIR TCAS 3 general-knowledge paper. Preparation should therefore rest on generic, well-supported learning principles (retrieval practice, spacing, summarisation) applied to a core politics and IR syllabus plus current affairs.

### Cited Findings

- Thammasat admissions documents list BIR (Political Science, International Politics) round 3 score statistics from older cycles, with contact bir.thammasat@gmail.com. No exam content specification was found. — [TU Admissions PDF (2020)](https://www.tuadmissions.in.th/img/2020051119442144.pdf); [TU Admissions PDF (2025)](https://www.tuadmissions.in.th/img/2025112701490623.pdf)
- Writing Next ranks summarisation of texts second of 11 writing interventions, so summarising readings serves both components. — [AdLit](https://www.adlit.org/topics/writing/summary-writing-next)

### Inferences

- Build a knowledge organiser of core concepts (sovereignty, realism, liberalism, constructivism, security dilemma, regimes, the UN system, ASEAN, democracy and regime types, the Thai constitution and institutions, major wars and treaties, globalisation and political economy). Drill it with spaced retrieval (flashcards or low-stakes quizzes) weekly from now to 21 Mar 2027, which gives about 22 weeks.
- Add weekly current-affairs reading (international and Thai) with one-paragraph summaries. These double as essay evidence banks and interview material.

### Gaps

- The retrieval-practice and spacing meta-analyses (e.g. Adesope et al. 2017; Cepeda et al. 2006; Dunlosky et al. 2013) were not retrieved this session. They are well established but uncited here.
- No past BIR or Thai political-science general-knowledge papers were found. The 2026–27 BIR announcement on tuadmissions.in.th or the BIR Facebook page should be checked for the official syllabus.
